Down by the riverside, fiery arrows fly toward Nemuri Kyoshiro. Several ninja emerge from the shadows and encircle him. He kills six, but one escapes. Later, at Kitagawa Riverside Teahouse, a pickpocket named Kinpachi drops off a note from Chen Sun to Nemuri, telling Him to meet him at the Seikoji Temple in Kameido tomorrow. Nemuri asks if the police are cleaning up the riverside, but no corpses have been found there. He goes to bed with a geisha named Utakichi.

The following day, Lord Maeda of the Kaga Clan asks about Nemuri. He hired the ninjas. Not much is known about him, but he is clearly a skilled swordsman. The Kaga leader enlists Chisa, a young woman whom he took in as an orphan and employed. He also tells Negero that Chen Sun and all who side with Zeniya Gohei must die. He hopes Negero and Chisa can goad Nemuri into killing Chen Sun for them, then kill Nemuri.

Chisa calls upon Nemuri, claiming that Chen Sun wants to murder her. Apparently, Zeniya's people possess something that can destroy the Kaga Clan. Nemuri agrees, on the condition that Chisa sleeps with him. She offers him 100 gold instead, and he concedes, saying that the sex proposal was a test of her valor. He asks Mojiwara, who runs the teahouse, to watch over Chisa and gives him the gold.

At Seikoji Temple, Nemuri meets Chen Sun, whose ancestor, Chen Gen-Ping, came from the Ming Court to Japan in the Tensho Era and introduced Shorenji Boxing to Japan. Chen Sun comes to Edo to punish the Kaga Clan and avenge Zeniya, a powerful merchant who partnered with the Kaga Clan leader to trade with foreigners illegally. When the Shogunate caught on, Lord Maeda had Zeniya killed. Chen Sun was his partner. He also reveals that Chisa is Lord Maeda's spy.

Who can Nemuri trust? What secret will lead to Lord Maeda's demise? Is Zeniya Gohei truly dead, or is another puppeteer pulling the strings behind this charade?

<blockquote><i>Me? I'm just someone who's angry at all mankind, even though I'm a man myself. I'm past the point of no return. I know not what my future holds, but in the time that I have, I shall be the ruin of the evil men that cross my path.</i></blockquote>

Whoa, boy, this movie has a lot of plot. Since so little is written about the story and its characters, I added more detail than I usually do, but it still covers only the first act. Despite appearances, this movie leans more toward a detective story than an outright action flick. Yes, there are fights, but the movie's intricate plot, secret lairs, and mysteries take center stage.

At the center is the ultimate MacGuffin: a jade Buddha statuette. Hidden inside is what will seal Kaga's fate. Thematically, the film explores greed and how it brings ruin. If Lord Maeda or Zeniya Goehi gave up their greedy plans, they could live, and live profitably. But as with the wealthy, too much is never enough.

The cinematography is elegant, and the lighting does an excellent job of setting the mood for each scene. I only have a low-quality version of this movie, so I can only imagine how good a remaster would look. To my knowledge, no boutique labels have picked this one up. Perhaps it's not exploitative enough for them.

Nemuri is, at times, a bit of a playboy, but ultimately has a code. He cannot be bought, especially when, like many heroes in these stories, he knows the power of his training and can use it for protection or slaughter. Throughout the story, flashbacks to his training show how his past informs his present choices.

<blockquote><i>For me, the dark world of humanity, not the pure and just world of nature, is where I belong. To that world, where every man is a ronin, I must return.</i></blockquote>

That said, I wouldn't say anyone has particularly complex characterization. The women, in particular, are severely underserved, as most are jealous, petty geishas. Chisa begins as a femme fatale of sorts, but the story reduces her to a love interest

What a racist English title. The Japanese is "Adventures of Nemuri Kyoshiro," but I guess they thought English speakers wouldn't see a movie with a dude's name in it. You know, like <i>Captain Sinbad</i>, <i>Hud</i>, <i>Johnny Cool</i>, or any other movie with a dude's name in it that came out the same year in the US.

From my understanding, this series gets more violent and exploitative as it goes. But this one retains a more refined tone that may not suit its target audience. Still, I appreciate a twisty detective story.
