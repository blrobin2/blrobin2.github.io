<b>Cult Movie Challenge 2019 | 9/52 | Dario Argento</b>

Franco Arnò, aka Cookie, walks the street at night, blind and aided by his niece, Lori. They pass a car where someone threatens to blackmail an unseen person. Cookie stops to tie his shoe, whispering to Lori to look at who was in the car.  Back home, after Lori is asleep, Cookie has a vision of someone being hit over the head, bleeding out. We see from the attacker's perspective as they dump the body, the nightwatchman for the Terzi Medical Institute. The attacker then breaks into the medical institute and sneaks into a doctor's office. Dr. Manera drives up to the gate, finds the guard incapacitated, and sees a shadow escape into the night.

The following day, Cookie takes a walk past the area. Reporter Carlo Giordani nearly knocks Cookie over. Cookie questions Carlo about what happened, only learning of the robbery. Carlo meets with Police Superintendent Spimi, who gives him the full rundown, including that nothing was stolen. Anna Terzi, daughter of Professor Fulvio Terzi, finds her father asking the DA, Chief of Police, and others to please avoid asking about his research, especially since nothing was stolen. Dr. Casoni, however, worries that whoever broke in may have committed espionage and photographed confidential documents.

Dr. Calabresi checks the lock on his drawer — the same one we saw the intruder approach. He makes a phone call to meet with someone at 5 that night. His fiancée, Bianca, arrives, and he confides in her that he knows what was stolen and who stole it. He arrives at the train station, where paparazzi await the arrival of a famous woman. We take the POV of the thief as he pushes Dr. Calabresi in front of an arriving train. A paparazzo catches the fall on film. The next day, Lori brings the paper to Cookie, telling him that the man she saw in the car the other night is dead. Carlo Giordani wrote the article.

Cookie has a hunch that he will bring to Carlo. Will it be the key to solving this mystery? As they investigate, they find nine leads and determine to follow each.

<blockquote><i>— If we could just latch onto one of those nine tails, we'd be on our way to solving the puzzle.
— Yeah, but which one?<br>
— We'd better hurry, because whoever it is is getting desperate.</i></blockquote>

The first act of the movie is tight! It's a great little mystery setup that I was fully on board for. The plot gets a tad dry as they follow all the leads, but it never loses my interest. I know this one is pretty dry compared to other Argentos. I probably enjoyed this one more than I expected because I haven't been watching much giallo as of late. That said, I had a good time! What can I say?

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- The young girl and older man with disability dynamic reminds me a bit of Argento's later movie, <i>Phenomena</i>.
- I love Cookie! He seems to have a second sight, conjuring images in his head. His personality is one of the most distinctive I can recall from an early Argento movie. Karl Malden, who plays Cookie, gives him an almost Jimmy Stewart vibe.
- The film uses a recurring motif of a close-up of a brown eye to signal that we are seeing through the murderer's perspective.
- Ennio Morricone never failed, man — this score rules.
- The kills are quite graphic and striking. Dr. Calabresi being pushed in front of the train was borderline slapstick, but shocking all the same. That said, it's not as graphic as anything in <i><a href="https://boxd.it/3QFG31">The Bird with the Crystal Plumage</a></i> or <i><a href="https://boxd.it/4gjva1">Four Flies on Grey Velvet</a></i>.
- "Marcel, for Christ's sake, I don't give a damn about your wife's ravioli!" Marcel proceeds to talk about the ravioli for the rest of the movie.
- The barber shave scene is funny!
- The plot has some problematic elements as it suggests that XYY syndrome leads to criminal tendencies.
- One of the more awkward sex scenes I've seen — negative chemistry
- The insult competition! Gigi the Loser is iconic.
- The single-line journal entries are… convenient.
- — "You're a real bastard." — "Naturally. Cops are all bastards. We get confessions out of people, take bribes, and oppress minorities."
- The set piece for the climax is stellar!
