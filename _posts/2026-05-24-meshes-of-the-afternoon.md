<a href="https://boxd.it/UBBe4" rel="nofollow"><b>Film Theory and Criticism | Rudolf Arnheim</b></a>

Maya Deren followed Arnheim's desire to create films that can exist only as films. By using the representational aspect of film, she creates abstractions that are juxtaposed to extend and distort the representational. An oblong shadow predicates the person. A key falls through multiple spaces only to land a couple of steps down. The camera shifts several facets of different spaces to create impossible interior geographies. The sequence of Maya chasing her shadow and mirror repeats — versions of herself accumulate, unable to act in space. And none of it is so sequestered from reality as a movie set, where elements can be carefully staged and articulated. The film invites the accidental aspects of nature — the wind blowing through her hair, the turning of a record player — to further ingrain the unreal with verisimilitude.

In short, the film is the perfect articulation of Arnheim's theories and is possibly the greatest short film ever made.
