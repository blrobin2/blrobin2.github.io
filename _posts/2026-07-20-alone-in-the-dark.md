Ten thousand years ago, the Abkani tribe opened a gate between our world and the world of darkness, then disappeared off the face of the Earth.

<b>22 Years Ago</b>

Twenty special children are selected from an orphanage. One goes missing. The orphanage reports that all twenty have gone missing. From the opening crawl, we know that the children are Sleepers, experimented on, merged with the darkness. That child hides in a high-voltage box, his vision full of monsters.

<b>Present</b>

That boy is now Edward Carby, telling a kid on a plane to be afraid of the dark. He used to be a paranormal investigator for Bureau 713, a covert group dedicated to finding Abkani artifacts and holding back the darkness.

<blockquote><i>Just because you can't see something doesn't mean it can't kill you.</i></blockquote>

James Pinkerton receives a call saying that Carnby has an artifact and that he must kill Carnby to claim it for himself. A taxi chase through the street market leads to a street fight. Carnby is stronger than Pinkerton, but the attacker gives him chase. Carnby steals a cop's gun and shoots the assailant, but it doesn't seem to slow him down. Finally, Carnby throws him on a spike.

Aline Cedrac opens a box with a mysterious Abkani tablet. Her boyfriend has been missing for three months. She works for Professor Lionel Hudgens, who is off looking for another Abkani artifact. At sea, his crew finds a gold-cased coffin. The crew overtakes him to open the coffin. Doing so unleashes something sinister. When Hudgens breaks out, he finds the crew dead. From the golden box, he claims another piece of the puzzle.

Meanwhile, Carnby's artifact wakes up and glows. The Sleepers stop what they're doing and walk into the night without a trace. What has been awakened? Can Carnby and Bureau 713 stop it?

Look, I've seen more entertaining bad movies, but this one isn't THAT bad. The acting is solid, and the story has enough of a mystery to keep me invested. Having Uwe Boll's budget certainly helps. I don't know if I saw the theatrical cut, because people are mentioning a sex scene that definitely wasn't in this one. So maybe that's why this is better than I expected.
