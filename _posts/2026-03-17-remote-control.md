<b>Earth 1987</b>

Alan rides a stationary bike in his briefs while channel surfing. Meanwhile, his wife, Julia, puts on some sci-fi-themed BDSM outfit. She mentions some tape she rented — a 1957 sci-fi flick called <i>Remote Control</i>. Alan puts the movie on. In the movie, it's the 1980s. Eva inserts a VHS-esque reel-to-reel into her View-O-Vision. On the VOV, the title card reads: Remote Control. Alan laughs at the coincidence that a movie from the 50s predicted VHS in the 80s. But when the movie comes on, it just shows Eva reflected back to herself. Voices from Eva's VOV call out her name, and her head feels strange. Her husband, Milo, enters to find Eva has disappeared. She approaches from behind and murders Milo with her futuristic knitting needles. When Eva looks to the camera, the image crossfades, and Alan sees himself on the screen. Julia enters, and she hears it call her name. She uses the bullwhip that accompanies her BDSM outfit and repeatedly whips Alan.

Cosmo works in a successful video rental store. He helps a woman named Belinda who is looking for Truffaut's <i>Stolen Kisses</i>, but it sounds too French to Cosmo. Cosmo's boss, Georgie, snaps him out of flirting with Belinda to help him set up a display rack that resembles the View-O-Vision from <i>Remote Control</i>, with a mirror instead of a screen. Some sales guy came by and offered the rental store free copies of <i>Remote Control</i> if they put up a display in their store. As soon as they plug in the display, folks crowd around it, excited. Allegra, a shop girl who has the hots for Cosmo, asks him for help finding something to watch. Cosma hands her <i>Remote Control</i>, and some guy flips out, demanding that he saw it first. Cosmo is dismayed to see Belinda approach the guy — the two are clearly together, as both have a sort of sci-fi-chic vibe. While Allegra rents the tape, the guy looks over her shoulder at the computer and notes her address.

<blockquote><i>Maybe looking at yourself in the mirror is the scariest thing you can do, but those people are dying to do it.</i></blockquote>

While closing up, Georgie finds a copy of <i>War of the Worlds</i>, which Allegra was pretending to look for. He suggests Cosmo deliver it to her, and Cosmo suggests Georgie. So, the two go together. Meanwhile, Allegra watches <i>Remote Control</i>. When she gets to the part where it reflects her back, she thinks she's on Candid Camera. The guy who freaked out at the store enters her bedroom. Allegra hears something calling out her name. The guy holds her down and laughs. Georgie and Cosmo arrive at Allegra's and see the guy making moves on Allegra. What they don't realize is that the guy is choking Allegra to death.

<blockquote><i>That don't look like any old sci-fi movie I've seen. They didn't have sex back then.</i></blockquote>

Where did this tape, <i>Remote Control</i>, come from? Will Cosmo and the gang realize what's going on before it's too late?

This movie is director Jeff Lieberman's most direct attempt to both lovingly mock a genre while also participating in it. In the 70s, he riffed on the "nature kills" genre with <a href="https://boxd.it/6gudf1"><i>Squirm</i></a>, which works because it doesn't let the riffing get in the way of the story. Here, he's being far more meta while still attempting to make a thrilling picture. It plays in the same realm as much of meta-horror today, fully acknowledging the genre while also being in it.

That's not to say that the movie is bad. But when you have one way to incite an incident, it gets a little old. Also, Kevin Dillon is the wrong type for the script they have. The guy needs to be a little dorky, which Dillon is, but he needs to pull off a line like, "It's murder one, babe," and it just comes across so silly. Also, they keep calling cops "muldoons."

I like the costume design! Everyone's doing the 80s sci-fi chic thing. At first, I thought it was just Belinda and her boyfriend, Victor, but we see folks throughout the movie who have that retro-futurism vibe.

The movie litters its screen with movies. Like, in one scene, it's a pile of <i>Magic</i>, <i>Scream and Die</i>, <i>Rumble Fish</i>, <i>My Tutor</i>, and <i>The Manipulator</i>. I understand <i>Rumble Fish</i> because Kevin's brother, Matt, is in it. Maybe the others are movies from the same studio or stuff that the crew of this movie also worked on.

Overall, I enjoyed the movie. It's at its most fun when the movie's events sync up with the mind-control movie-within-a-movie. They honestly could've made this movie 20 minutes shorter, and it would've been even better, but I know no one with an '80s budget was putting out anything that short.
