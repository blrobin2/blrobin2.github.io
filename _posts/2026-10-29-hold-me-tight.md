Clarisse plays a Polaroid matching game with family photos. She messes up, and angrily slaps them down, growling that she will start again.

Early one morning, Clarisse packs a few things, checks on her kids, Paul and Lucie, and leaves. Time seems to interweave as we cut between Clarisse's journey and what is happening back at the house. Clarisse's husband, Marc, sets up breakfast for Paul and Lucie and wonders where Clarisse is. Clarisse makes a grocery list and leaves. Lucie sits down at the piano.

Clarisse takes the car in the garage to the gas station. There, Clarisse talks to her friend, who jokes about Clarisse running away. Clarisse says she's just gonna drive. She says she wants to see the sea. The friend puts on new tires and checks the oil. Marc is worried, not knowing where Clarisse is.

Lucie's piano playing comes through the radio as Clarisse drives. She talks to the music, telling Lucie to stop playing scales and play something else. At home, Lucie stops and plays a song. Lucie and Clarisse seem to talk through Lucie's journal. At a bar, Clarisse gets drunk and talks to patrons about her family, wondering what Marc will tell the kids to explain her absence.

The film weaves together details and conversations as Clarisse starts a new life elsewhere.

<blockquote><i>I imagine that I left. Had to find something, right?</i></blockquote>

The film explores grief and the ways people move on (or don't). As time passes, Marc throws away Clarisse's perfumes. Paul sees this as Marc breaking Mom and throwing her away. Clarisse blows up on a tourist for reprimanding his fidgety child. Lucie writes in her journal — the same journal that Clarisse writes in.

The film has a soft, reflective quality. Everything seems to take place in some hyperreal space, where time and distance don't inhibit communication. Chunks of dialogue repeat in different locations, sometimes as echoes. Some moments play in Clarisse's head, and some we think are in her head aren't. Clarisse recreates rituals, assigning new people to play old roles.

I wish I saw this in theaters when it first came out. I feel like the emotional weight would've hit me harder. Still, there's plenty to appreciate in this stylish, if not slim, film.
