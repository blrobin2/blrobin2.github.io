Ah, Prague, the gateway to the west. A cleaner sweeps up debris in the park, chasing off a crow standing over an unconscious man. At the hospital, the doctors find no signs of injury. When they check the unconscious man's heartbeat, they find none and declare him dead from cardiac arrest. But we hear the man's voice in his head, still alive but unable to move or speak. They pull out the man's passport — an American journalist named Gregory Moore.

The morgue lists the man's effects, finding it strange that Gregory's body is still warm. Still, they throw him in a locker. There, Gregory tries to think through what happened to him that brought him to this point. A series of flashing images brings us back to when Gregory was still alive. He's in a Czech news office dictating a news story over the phone. Medics are carting off a dead man while Gregory tells his coworker Jacques that he's leaving early for a date with Mira.

At the train station, Gregory meets Mira, who has just arrived from the Soviet Union. After a day of touring the city, Gregory declares his intentions to leave for London and to smuggle Mira out of the city. After another series of jumbled images, we find Gregory and Mira at a ritzy party with artists, politicians, and journalists. Several men admire Mira. Gregory and Jacques's peer, Jessica, is jealous of Mira and unsuccessfully attempts to seduce Gregory.

Later that night, Jacques calls on Gregory — another political official has taken a fall. But by the time Gregory arrives on the scene, Jacques informs him that it was a case of mistaken identity. When Gregory returns to the apartment, he finds Mira's bed empty but no sign of her. All of her personal effects are still there, including her money and passport. Where is Mira? What happened to Gregory? Even if he remembers, will he be able to break out of whatever has paralyzed him in time?

I suppose the gore hounds are disappointed in a giallo without the customary gore, judging by some of the scores I've seen. Still, the film contains more tension than many gialli I've seen, while still offering a journey into the darkness beneath the glimmer. The transitions between past and present through the image flashes are so intriguing — they create just enough suspense without spoiling the story's direction. That said, I can also see horror fans feeling restless throughout this movie's slower pace. We see dead bodies throughout, but few, if any, stalking about or people BEING murdered.

The film makes excellent use of Prague, Zagreb, and Ljubljana to give it a distinctly Central European vibe. Prague starts as the symbol of individual freedom, where government foibles don't interfere with the common person's life, but in that freedom comes apathy when something serious happens. The police and other authorities are uninterested in helping Gregory find Mira, as they have their own internal world to maintain.

The film also touches on the wealthy and the predatory way with which they view the world around them. In one scene, while Gregory is talking to someone who saw Mira at the party, the man picks a tomato and offers it to Gregory, saying, "Go ahead, you won't hear it crying." There are other examples, but I'll leave those for the viewer to discover.

While this film leaves something to be desired at times, it ends with a bang that makes the journey worth it.
