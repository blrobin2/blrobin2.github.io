<b><a href="https://boxd.it/q5w9E" rel="nofollow">Criterion Challenge 2021 | 31/52 | Directed by Charlie Chaplin</a></b>

Charlie Chaplin sings Swing High as the Ringmaster's step-daughter swings on the trapeze. The Ringmaster chastises her for missing the rope again, pushing her into a prop and denying her supper. The clowns gather around, and the Ringmaster yells at them for being unfunny.

Meanwhile, the Tramp wanders the outskirts of the circus, broke and hungry as usual. A pickpocket takes a man's wallet and pawns it off on the unknowing Tramp to avoid suspicion. The pickpocket tries to get the wallet back, but a police officer catches the pickpocket and gives the wallet back to the Tramp. But the original owner spots the Tramp holding the wallet and sics the police on him.

The chase takes them through a Mirror Maze and into the center ring of the circus, complete with a magic trick, and slapstick falls that draw big laughs from the crowd. The clowns run out, but the crowd boos, demanding the return of "the funny man." And so, the Ringleader chases the Tramp down and offers him a job.

The film follows the Tramp as he tries to survive life in the circus.

After the big productions of <a href="https://boxd.it/97fi1V" rel="nofollow"><i>The Gold Rush</i></a>, the deceptively simple premise of the circus leads to much more entertaining sight gags and visual tricks. In many ways, the film is an homage to the origins of his physical comedy — this includes performances of classic clown gags that, of course, the Tramp subverts through his bumbling and misunderstanding. The tightrope walk finale is truly stunning work.

The film also features the myriad ways in which capitalists will exploit your labor, especially when they know they have something good on their hands. It is only when the Tramp knows that he is the reason the circus is succeeding that he can negotiate a fair wage and help out the Ringmaster's step-daughter. But of course, if performance slips even a bit, the livelihood is on the line again.

The Tramp's last full-length outing of the silent film era was also Chaplin's most difficult production (see divorce, back taxes, fires, etc.) In some ways, the circus leaving at the end is a metaphor for the end of the silent film eta.

The movie may also be my favorite Chaplin. While <a href="https://boxd.it/5OkjqH" rel="nofollow"><i>Modern Times</i></a> is a better movie, this one has the most consistent laughs and cohesive story.
