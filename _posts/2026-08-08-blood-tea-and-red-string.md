The film feels like a folk tale that took psychedelics.

I could see multiple readings of its meaning, none necessarily mutually exclusive. In one sense, it seems to be about the passive role women are expected to play in the lives of men, fulfilling whatever desire or fantasy. With the presence of the human, you could also read it as her being the creatures' god, and the bat-crows are honoring her by creating a child for her, and the rats try to do it through mimicking her tea ceremonies. Their disagreement on what to do with the representation of the god that the bat-crows made feels like different sects fighting over which way is correct, even though each is representing different facets of the same multifaceted being.

The stop-motion jank feels intentional in the way it gives the story an added uncanny layer. The sound design was immersive, giving us further clues into the world and creating eloquent transitions between spaces and scenes.

I don't think the film has one coherent, objective meaning, so read into it what you will. At the very least, the film is certainly a bizarre, uncomfortable, and fairly singular experience.
