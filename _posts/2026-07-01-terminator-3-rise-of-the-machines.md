The future has not been written. At the end of <i><a href="https://boxd.it/eZyODV">T2</a></i>, John and Sarah Connor stop Judgment Day, destroying all instances of the Terminator. Judgment Day 1997 does not come. But Judgment Day is inevitable, I guess. And John Connor has a destiny, no matter what he does. So, uh, I guess the future has been written.

The CGI is dated, but it is also poorly implemented, giving us no sense of impact or scale. Nothing feels like it has consequences. The film also leans way harder into the comedic elements introduced in <i>T2</i>, knowing it has no way to awe the audience the way that <i>T2</i> did. Arnold is quippier than ever. The silly sound effects and cartoonish CGI make for an impactless, tedious experience. Also, not bringing back Brad Fiedel and his electronic score makes this movie all the more generic.

This film is not the first attempt to continue the story after <i>T2</i>, which belongs to a now-defunct Universal Studios ride. This film kicks off another continuity that continues through <a href="https://boxd.it/2abQ"><i>Terminator: Salvation</i></a>. Each subsequent project attempted to reboot the series in some way, the most recent being the James Cameron-blessed <i><a href="https://boxd.it/f7c6d3">Dark Fate</a></i>. While underperformance and bankruptcies were the primary contributors to this reboot hell, the underlying cause is that <i>T2</i>, already silly but quite beloved, closed the loop and completed everything <a href="https://boxd.it/ePvyHB"><i>T1</i></a> had initiated.

And yet, franchises gonna franchise.

I wouldn't call this movie bad so much as misguided and barely motivated beyond profit.

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- The new lady terminator (not to be confused with the wonderfully bad <i><a href="https://boxd.it/cAk">Lady Terminator</a></i>) appears IN A CLOTHING STORE, but still steals another woman's clothes and Lexus. Is this a dumb axe to grind? Yes. Will I stop? No.
- The T-800, looking much older, goes to a club on ladies' night and gets his outfit from a leather-clad stripper.
- Kate Brewster, who hooked up with John in high school, gives us the rundown of the previous movie.
- Kate's US Lieutenant General dad, Robert, doesn't like her current fiancé because, I guess, he's not hardened by life and trauma, so clearly she's gonna end up with John.
- So many men tell women to shut up in this movie.
- "Talk to the Hand" is not funny either time.
- Killing Sarah off with leukemia offscreen is dull.
- The Dr. Silberman cameo is pointless.
- "Do you wanna live? Come on!" has less of a poetic cadence than "Come with me if you want to live."
- A bullet-riddled hearse labeled "Valley of Peace" is kind of funny.
- So, it was "always" about Robert Brewster, and everything in <i>T2</i> was ultimately meaningless, as the military was already doing what Miles was?
- The T-1 is pretty neat, obviously influenced by ED-209 in <i>RoboCop</i>.
- John says Kate reminds him of his mother— is this when she falls in love with him??
- What can stop T-X? A strong magnet. Or does she??
- Why does John set the detonator before they even make it down?
- I like the twist that Robert sent them to a fallout shelter, knowing there was no way to stop Skynet.
- So, the Terminator's lesson ISN'T "the future has not been written," but "Don't stop fighting."
