<b><a href="https://boxd.it/q5w9E/detail">Criterion Challenge 2021 | 51/52 | Bruce Lee: His Greatest Hits</a></b>

<b>Shanghai, 1908</b>

On a rainy afternoon, Chen Zhen arrives outside the Jingwu School. As he enters, he sees the altar surrounded by garlands. His master, Huo Yuanjia, has died. Upon seeing the grave site, Chen Zhen breaks down, unable to accept it. The officiant knocks him out. Two days pass. Chen Zhen cannot bring himself to eat. The others believe that Huo Yuanjia died of pneumonia, but Chen isn't so sure.

Wu En, translator for Hiroshi Suzuki, arrives. Suzuki runs the Japanese Hongkou school. Wu gives the students a banner reading "Sick Man of Asia," referring to Huo Yuanjia, and calls the Chinese "weaklings" compared to the Japanese. He asks for a challenger, stirring Chen Zhen's ire, but the elder students stop him, saying they only fight for peace.

Later, Chen Zhen brings the gifted banner back to the Hongkou school. The sensei laughs, but Chen Zhen offers to fight any student who is willing. Suzuki's protégé makes a move, but Chen takes him down easily. The other students rise to fight. Chen removes his shirt and takes them all. He then makes them eat the banner.

The film follows the repercussions of Chen Zhen's actions, as well as the investigation to determine who really killed Huo Yuanjia.

The film explores Japanese colonialism during China's century of humiliation. The "Sick Man of Asia" here is a reference to the century of humiliation and applies it specifically to Japan's bullying of China. Chen Zhen tries to enter a Japanese-created park that forbids dogs and Chinese people (based on Huangpu Park). He fights the Japanese fighters with their own weapons.

But Chen Zhen learns through the film that revenge does not resolve the issue—in fact, it makes it worse. His fiancée, Yuan Li'er, worries that his journey will drive them further apart and that their future home and children are in jeopardy. Still, Chen Zhen persists, for what other choice does he have?

The film focuses on legitimate kung fu, but occasionally dips into wuxia-esque stuff, like "swinging fighters around like nunchucks." The sound design has a couple of striking moments where they emphasize a single punch with added layers of ambiance, giving it weight. Also, Bruce Lee wears so many disguises, giving the film its comedic breaks.

Overall, the film's emotional core and political bent help it stand out. And, of course, Bruce Lee's charisma.
