<blockquote><i>You ever hear the saying, "Monsters aren't born — they're made?"</i></blockquote>Linda from Planning & Strategy has been working nights and weekends for this company. She's given them the best years of her life, and recent hire Donovan has taken all the credit. At home, all she has is her bird, Sweetie, and her beloved Survivor, which she hopes to be on someday. But things are looking up for Linda! The recently passed CEO promised her a Vice President position at the company when his son, Bradley, takes over.

That is, until she finds out that Bradley is promoting Donovan to the position. Linda charges into Bradley's office for an explanation, and he gives it to her simply: she's unkept, covered in food stains, and not really a people person. Bradley concocts a way to get her help managing a merger by inviting her on a company flight to Bangkok under the pretense that she can prove Bradley wrong and get the position.

On the flight, it becomes apparent just how little Bradley and the other senior execs think of her. Suddenly, the plane hits a severe storm and crashes into the ocean. Linda survives, washing up on the shore of some South Asian island. The only other survivor she finds is Bradley. Will the two be able to reconcile their differences and find a way off the island?

<blockquote><i>No one is coming to save you.</i></blockquote>The film rides on the shoulders of Rachel McAdams tremendous performance. Dylan O'Brien is also excellent, don't get me wrong, but McAdams is the cornerstone on which this movie is built.

It's a Sam Raimi thriller, so the movie has plenty of gross-out moments. I don't know how he pulls them off, because they are disgusting but also funny and don't induce nausea the way some gross-out stuff does with me.

Best of all, the movie takes its time to develop characterization. Some folks complain about the slow second act, but without those beats, the climax would not have hit as hard. The movie <b>uses</b> what we learn about the characters beyond just eliciting sympathy, but to make the story work — a note I would give to<b> so many</b> horror films.

The film's biggest flaw is its neoliberal politics. It takes some ideas from movies like <i>Triangle of Sadness</i> and... I don't want to say too much and spoil anything, accidentally or otherwise.

Still, we don't get movies with this sort of craftsmanship, especially for a mostly original movie (as in, not based on IP). While it isn't as high octane as <i>Drag Me to Hell</i>, this movie is the closest to it that Sam Raimi has directed since. See it, and get a healthy dose of groans and laughs, sometimes in the same beat.
