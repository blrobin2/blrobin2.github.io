As someone who only watched other people play Backrooms-adjacent games, I can say that the movie captures the games' atmosphere quite effectively. I enjoyed the performances — Chiwetel Ejiofor refuses to phone it in, and we thank him for it — it’s nice to see Renate Reinsve in something Joachim Trier didn't direct (my fault for not seeing more of those than Reinsve for not being in them).

What the movie falls a bit short on for me isn't unique to it, but it keeps me from loving it as much as others do. So much horror these days is about trauma/grief — this movie is also one of those. But it also shows one of the leads in therapy for grief, saying the literal meaning of the movie out loud. I suppose I wanted a little more subtlety in the approach. I'll save the rest for the spoiler section below.

Shout out to the guy sitting next to us who, when the credits started, said, "That was the worst movie I've ever seen in my life." I'm going to take you at your word and bless your eyes, so virgin and pure.

#### SPOILERS

The backrooms, as I understand it, are a map of the world's collective memory, articulated through liminal space and shards of remembrance. Every memory formed has at least one corresponding place in the backrooms, perhaps recreated each time it is recalled, and distorted as memories are altered over time.

Clark, in therapy, relives the night his wife kicked him out, over and over, for him to see the path he could have taken and may take in the future. But ultimately, Clark's journey is about the steadfast refusal to change, and his anger literally eating him alive.

Mary, as the therapist, sees the answer but cannot hand it to him. He has to come to the conclusion himself, or he won't accept it. She becomes trapped in his backrooms, trying to save him but learning that some people cannot be saved, thereby liberating her from the guilt of not saving her mother from her own fixations.

It's not obvious obvious, and looking at my score, it didn't hurt the movie that much, but it is painted out so clearly that I find it difficult to interpret it any other way. That said, I will be reading other reviews and looking for fresh perspectives.
