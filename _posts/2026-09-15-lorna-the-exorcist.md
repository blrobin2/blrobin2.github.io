For her 18th birthday, Linda plans to go on a trip with her parents, Patrick and Marianne, to St. Trop. Suddenly, a woman named Lorna calls Patrick and demands that they meet in Camargue. So, Patrick changes plans, confounding Linda and Marianne. He claims it's for business, but Linda knows it's to meet one of his girlfriends.

A doctor brings a Jane Doe into his office, trying to determine her identity. She recently awoke from a coma, but she is still disconnected from the world. The doctor believes someone is controlling her.  Back in her room, she cries out for Lorna. Lorna's voice tells her she's been condemned to the void for betraying her.

Who is Lorna, and what does she have in mind for Linda and her family? Probably, like, sex stuff. Or, like, definitely sex stuff.

Franco's career was in a bad place by this point in the 70s, and his movies became more softcore than horror in an attempt to draw a new clientele. When this version flopped, the producers recut the movie, removing the scant remnants of plot to make it purely softcore.

The "sex scenes" are primarily Lorna and Linda licking each other's pubic hair. Apparently, anyone actually experiencing anything resembling pleasure was a bridge too far. Their scenes just seem to happen mid-movie, without any context. For at least two scenes, the characters haven't met each other yet.

Mostly, it's just boring, existing in some horror/porno purgatory, never willing to commit to either. Most of the movie is people walking around, talking on the phone, or gambling.

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- Franco makes a cameo as the doctor trying to diagnose the Jane Doe.
- One of Lorna's curses is making crabs crawl around on Marianne's vulva.
- The incest angle is gross! It leads to the movie's one (supremely justified) kill.
