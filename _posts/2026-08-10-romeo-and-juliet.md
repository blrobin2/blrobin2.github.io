<blockquote><i>Two households, both alike in dignity, in fair Verona where we lay our scene, from ancient grudge break to new mutiny, where civil blood makes civil hands unclean. From forth the fatal loins of these two foes, a pair of star-crossed lovers take their life, whose misadventured piteous overthrows do with their death bury their parents' strife.</i></blockquote>

Some Capulet boys wander the market in the streets of Verona, kicking hens and making a ruckus. From across the way, they see some Montagues and stir trouble, and a brawl breaks out between the rival houses. That is, until the Prince arrives on the scene, who's sick of their stupid quarrel and threatens their lives if they should quarrel again.

After the quarrel, Lady Montague seeks her teenage son, Romeo, whom Benvolio saw wandering as though in a daydream. They spot him coming up the street, a flower in hand. Meanwhile, Lord Capulet feels as though his daughter, Juliet, still behaves as a child, despite being 14 years old. Lord Capulet plans a masquerade ball for the evening, inviting guests from all over.

After a night out with the bros, Romeo decides to crash the masquerade ball. While surveying the party for beautiful women, he sets his eyes on Juliet dancing with another lord. Like a lovestruck teenager, Romeo is struck with, um, love! But, uh oh, she's a Capulet, and he's a Montague. How's that gonna work??

<blockquote><i>Did my heart love til now? Foreswear it sight, for I ne'er saw true beauty til this night.</i></blockquote>

The film is noteworthy, in part, because it actually cast teenagers in the roles of Romeo and Juliet, creating a similar stir that <i>Romeo + Juliet</i> created in the 90s, complete with fidelity to Shakespeare's script. Because the actors were teenagers, their lines were tweaked, giving them fewer speeches and more reaction shots to capture what those speeches may have captured.

My biggest joy while watching the movie is seeing some actors I've only seen as older folks making their breakthroughs, such as Olivia Hussey as Juliet and Michael York as Tybalt. There is a reason we don't see much Leonard Whiting beyond this role. He's perfectly green for a Romeo type, but, like many young actors, he was a bet that didn't necessarily pan out later. John McEnery's Mercutio set the standard for greasy fuckbois.

While <i>Romeo + Juliet</i> is probably the most fun adaptation of the story, this film is likely the best balance of accuracy and filmmaking.
