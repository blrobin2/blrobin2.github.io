I think it's about death.

I watched this movie several times in college, showing it to folks — I believe I was still religious at the time, based on who I showed it to. At some point after, I lost my faith, but never returned to see this until now. Today, the atheist stand-in is a little cartoonish in his apathy for humanity, what with the threatening SA and watching people die.

Still, the film is the perfect translation of a parable into a cinematic experience. I get the sense that Antonius Block is the director, setting a stage of differing ideologies, bringing his questions and grieving the silence. But what helps me, and perhaps the director, is Jof, who could see the invisible thread of reality while still being present as a loving father and doting husband.
