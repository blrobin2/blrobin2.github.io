<blockquote><i>You still don't get it, do you? He'll find her. That's what he does! That's all he does! You can't stop him!</i></blockquote>

I saw this movie so often as a child that it might be my most-watched film of all time. I didn't ask for it — my dad had a bootleg VHS of Schwarzenegger movies that was on all the time. I think <i>Red Heat</i> was on there too, as well as <i>Commando</i> and <i>Total Recall</i>.

I love the blend of sci-fi and slasher, even if the sci-fi leans conservative in its "one special person" perspective on historical progress and immediate assumption of slavery from the machines. I understand that, from a storytelling perspective, it makes the story focused and the motivations immediate. Still, as a world-building foundation, it's hilariously limited, which is why the Terminator cinematic universe has bent itself over backward to justify subsequent movies.

Of course, there are more progressive political interpretations. A private corporation working as a subcontractor for the government destroys the world with their war tech. The future provides an opportunity for white audiences to empathize with people who live in war-torn countries and the types of people it creates. But it's the answers to solving those issues that ultimately pull this movie politically right.

The choice to make the Terminator a cyborg is brilliant on a few levels. The obvious one is budget — easier to film a dude running around. Another is strategy — hidden in plain sight. But it also creates opportunities where the Terminator must stop for maintenance. Those moments help pace the story so it's not a constant dash. And, of course, the slow slip of the mask so that the killer is still not fully revealed until the finale.

The tension-building is as perfect as it gets. The timing and pacing are sharp, and every interaction makes an impact.

Harlan Ellison sued James Cameron for plagiarism, as the story seems to borrow elements wholesale from Ellison's script for <i>The Outer Limits</i> episode "Soldier," which Cameron admitted in interviews. So, the later versions of the movie have an "acknowledgment" of Ellison's works in the closing credits.

- I have never realized that Lance Henriksen is in this!
- I forgot how "80s rendition of manic pixie dream girl" Sarah is, with her pet iguana and moped.
- Milk propaganda!
- It's amazing how many issues Sarah's roommate listening to a Walkman causes in the movie.
- The murder scenes are horrifying! It has none of the slick style of a slasher kill.
- I wish convincing Sarah didn't take so much physical coercion.
- The eye surgery scene absolutely horrified me as a child. Why did my dad not only let me see this but watch it so many times??
- It's so funny how "I'll be back" is so iconic for Schwarzenegger. That scene does rule though — Terminator running through 30 cops.
- I have to imagine this movie inspired at least one public shooting.
- The Christ / Virgin Mary metaphor is amusing (JC / John Conner, etc)
- I also forgot how many flashbacks there are
- Kyle comes on pretty strong with the "I have always loved you, Sarah" bit, but hey, it works. We also get the piano ballad of the theme.
- Sarah's bold to pull a "think fast" on a man bred in military conflict.
- I love that, while Kyle gives Sarah the info dump she needs, it is ultimately her who saves the day.
- The Terminator claymation is pretty sick.
- I like that the pipe hitting the Terminator creates an almost musical tone.
- I have to admit, my eyes glaze over a little bit during the final showdown.
