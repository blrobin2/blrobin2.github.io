<a href="https://boxd.it/4JZDPh" title="When I first watched this movie">When I first watched this movie</a>, I felt the ominous vibe, but I couldn't put a theme into words. Even still, I felt the horror of Carol's helplessness and deep sadness as life felt increasingly unsafe for her.

This time around, I saw a few things:

1. Carol is obviously sick. While they never state what is wrong with her, I believe she has contracted AIDs and that her husband gave it to her. The first scene of the movie is her husband having sex with her, as well as the mental disconnect between them. Her husband grows angry with her sickness in large part because she cannot give him sex.

2. I don't have to tell you that AFAB people receive <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9291436/" rel="nofollow">significantly worse medical treatment</a> on average. Carol's doctor is quick to assume that all of her symptoms are psychosomatic, and her husband is more ready to believe the male doctor than his wife. If they had been willing to keep looking and to believe Carol, they might have caught what was wrong with her.

3. Because Carol does not get the medical care she needs, she feels drawn to this cult that can capture her symptoms but roots the cause in self-hatred. Her sickness, her inability to find a cure, is, according to this group and in true self-help form, Carol's fault. The leader himself claims to have AIDs and that his program has cured him of it.

4. The only solution to keeping Carol "safe" is to contain her in smaller, more restrictive spaces: the home, the cult's land, and finally, the igloo where another member died.
