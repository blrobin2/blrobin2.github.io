The film takes something as innocuous as footage of horses running in water and turns it into an abstraction. The sound design feels alien, syncing with motions and gestures to suggest galloping or broken carousels. In other words, the film challenges Arnheim's notion that sound in film adds only verisimilitude and detracts from artistic endeavors.

The footage is slow motion and ultra-close-up, creating a claustrophobic composition that never really breathes. Segments repeat like dream loops, and footage is assembled out of order, further disorienting the viewer and heightening anxiety.

Understandably, one piece that may bother folks is when the horses are running through fire. The filmmakers likely set the fire to "enhance" the nightmare aspect. While the footage is gorgeous, it is upsetting to see the horses in peril.
