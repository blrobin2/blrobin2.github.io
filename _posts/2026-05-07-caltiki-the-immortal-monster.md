<b><a href="https://boxd.it/qa26g">Cult Movie Challenge 2019 | 14/52 | 50s Sci-Fi</a></b>

A nearby volcano erupts, shooting rock across the landscape. An archaeologist, Nieto, stumbles through the clouds of ash, returning to his camp near Tikal, a dead city that once belonged to the Mayans. He collapses in shock before Dr. John Fielding and his wife, Ellen. Nieto had gone with Ulmer to explore a cave near the volcano, but Ulmer is nowhere to be found. Nieto comes out of shock briefly to cry out the name Caltiki. Curious, John forms a group, and they set out for the cave.

Ellen and Linda look after Nieto while the men search the cave for Ulmer. They find a break in the wall from the volcanic eruption. Behind them, carved stairs descend deeper into the cave. At the base stands a large statue of Caltiki, and a pool of water where priests drowned the humans sacrificed to the goddess. Max's Geiger counter emits a furious scratching sound, indicating high levels of radioactivity. They find Ulmer's camera, but not Ulmer.

Back at the camp, they set up a projector to watch Ulmer's footage, captured by one of the indigenous guides who went with them. On it, they see Nieto holding jewels found near the lake, only to drop the jewelry and unload his gun into someone or something off-screen. The natives perform a ritual to ward off evil spirits. Bob, one of the expedition members, decides to film it despite Linda's warning that it's bad luck.

The group decides to fetch diving gear and explore the water beneath the statue. Bob descends, finding jewelry among the bones. He tries to fill a bag with it, but something attacks him. The group pulls him up and finds his face eaten away.

After a successful collaboration with director Riccardo Freda on <i>I Vampiri</i>, for which Maro Bava served as cinematographer, Freda drops this movie early to let Bava take over. Whether it was a kind gesture of a friend or a coward abandoning ship is up to who you ask.

One thing is certain — the cinematography has a flare that helps it stand out amid the onslaught of sci-fi coming out in the 50s. The lighting is dramatic, breathing life into each scene. The special effects range wildly in quality, from impressively grotesque melty kills to an unconvincing blob monster.

The blob monster is probably the film's weakest aspect. Luckily, most of the movie has a smaller version of the monster that looks much better. Also, shooting in monochrome gets some added mileage from the effects. And, even though the miniatures aren't always convincing, I'll always have a soft spot for them.

The film sets up some standard love-triangle stuff. John and Ellen fight because Ellen was not expecting the expedition to be so rough and dangerous. Max tries to seduce Ellen, though Ellen is uninterested. Linda pursues Max, even though Max thinks very little of her (he has some racist things to say that I won't type).

After the first act, the film slows down a bit into talky sci-fi, but I don't mind that much. It never strays too far from goopy weirdness.
