<b><a href="https://boxd.it/q5w9E/detail" rel="nofollow">Criterion Challenge 2021 | 42/52 | Samurai</a></b>

<b>1874, Kanagawa Prefecture</b>

A baby's cry. The bars of a women's prison. Snow falls outside. Inmates gather around Sayo, the woman who just gave birth. She names the child Yuki, or Snow.

<b>Twenty years later</b>

A purple umbrella walks along city walls. A cart stops in front of her — Shibayama and his gang. She cuts them down, cutting off arms and necks. In one swift motion, she takes out Shibayama. With Shibayama dead, she returns to Matseuemon, who asked her to take Shibayama out. In return, Yuki asks for the location of three people: Banzō Takemura, Okono Kitahama, and Gishirō Tsukamoto.

<b>1873</b>

Four  people, including the three mentioned, killed Sayo's husband and son, then SAed her. Sayo killed one of them, Shokei Tokuichi, and was arrested while tracking down the others. So, she slept with anyone she could so she could give birth to a strong child to complete her vengeance.

<b>1882</b>

At age 8, Yuki trains with a martial arts master to become an asura so she can finish what her mother started.

<blockquote><i>You will carry on my vendetta, my poor child. You are my asura demon.</i></blockquote>

The film is based on a manga of the same name, and it features frames from the manga after a writer takes on writing Lady Snowblood's story. Toho wanted to capitalize on Meiko Kaji's success in the <i>Female Prisoner Scorpion</i> series. She also performed the movie's theme song.

The film is highly stylized, with incredible cinematography. The reds are striking, and the blood shoots in fountains. The fights are rarely long, with Yuki's sword tearing through folks with ease. The movie contains some of the most iconic shots I've seen.

The story is nonlinear, moving to several different points in time, spiraling around Yuki's birth as the pivot point. The story takes place during a period of upheaval, as Japan rushed to adopt more Western politics, military practices, and educational systems in an effort to modernize more rapidly.

In many ways, Yuki represents the dying old ways, trying to save Japan from itself. The targets represent the betrayal of Japan to itself, with positions in police and military operations, carrying guns. In one pivotal moment, a target hangs from two flags: the USA and Japan. The target pulls down the Japanese flag.

But the sun also rises. And this movie has a sequel.
