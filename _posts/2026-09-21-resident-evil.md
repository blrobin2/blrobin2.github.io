<b>Men would rather take mysterious medical supplies into Raccoon City than go to therapy.</b>

Zach Cregger's horror movies up to this point have done a tremendous job of setting up a concept, putting a flat, familiar story in an unconventional structure, and ultimately devolving into cruel, gory theatrics. Here, the film drops the unconventional structure, forcing the writers to make a straightforward narrative work. And wouldn't you know it? This movie is Cregger's best to date.

The fan service is rampant, but cleverly integrated so it doesn't feel like the story stops for its own sake. The pacing is strong, with a satisfying blend of humor, jump scares, and moments to breathe. Austin Abrams is a great "incompetent but driven" lead who moves forward when he needs to, but says "nope" when things get too out of hand.

The film explores a world where ineptitude runs from top to bottom, and everyone looks for someone else to take the blame. What a wild and improbable concept, right? Right? Bryan is running away from a pregnant girlfriend and the potential responsibility of growing up. The Umbrella Corporation is running away from their hand in all of this, trying to put the lid back on Pandora's box.

The ending is supremely unsatisfying, going for ambiguity (and potential sequels) over completing the story's arc. I felt the air of excitement leave my body as the ending title card came up. I don't know if Cregger will sign up for another one, but with how well this is doing, I'm sure a sequel is already in the pipeline.
