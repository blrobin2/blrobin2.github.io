HK Chan Wing fights Thai Bu Bo in the Sino-Thai Boxing Competition in Hong Kong. Blood and spittle sprays as the two trade blows before Wing kicks a hole into Bo's head. The ref calls it for Wing, but Bo catches Wing offguard and hits him repeatedly in the back of the head, breaking Wing's cervical vertebrae and paralyzing him. Later, Uncle Seven comes to see Hung, Wing's brother, as the club his gang manages. The Mainlander wants to meet with Hung alone. Hung agrees, but asks his men to come armed and wait outside in case there's any trouble.

The following day, Hung arrives at the warehouse, where Mainlander declares his intent to take all of Hung's gang's territory. A trap: Uncle Seven and Hung's men are dead. While Mainlander's men torture Hung, the spirit of a Buddhist monk appears. The rope ensnaring Hung snaps as the monk tells Hung to follow him. Hung looks around to find the men dead. Later that night, after having passionate sex with his girlfriend, a glowing orange arch summons him, but his girlfriend waking up sends it away.

Hung travels to Thailand to challenge Bo and avenge his brother. Bo agrees to a match in HK in three months. While riding down the river, Hung sees the glowing orange arch atop a Buddhist temple. The monks there know his name and expect his arrival. They tell him about their abbot Qing Zhao, who traveled to HK to defeat a black magic practitioners. I don't know how to describe this part — it's so wild. Anywho, one of the black magic guys manages to kill Qing before he achieves immortality.

So, Qing, trapped in his decaying body, calls on Hung to become a monk, defeat the black magicians, and help Qing finally achieve immortality. Will Hung be able to stop these black magicians?

This movie almost demands to be watched high. Alas, I am out of goodies, so I watched sober as a jaybird. The visuals are psychedelic without being cliché, refracting light and mirroring images in unusual ways. It's also exceptionally gross, with bloody head holes, festering wounds, melting flesh, eating and puking up animal parts, etc. I could really do without the animal cruelty and puke stuff.

It's wild how much this movie packs into its runtime. In a way, it feels like two totally disparate movies shoved together into one: a kickboxing revenge flick and a Buddhist-themed black magic extravaganza. I don't know if either half would constitute a full movie, and I don't really mind having both stories going at the same time. Of course, I find the black magic story way more interesting.

It's not high art, and it won't teach you anything about humanity, but it's a lot of fun. Yes, it's slow in places, but that's forgivable for the moments where it isn't.
