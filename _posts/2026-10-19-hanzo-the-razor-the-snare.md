Two thieves on the run, Hanzo on their trail. They attempt to run through a lord's procession, drawing the ire of Lord Okubo's retainers. Hanzo arrives in time to stop the retainers. The lord's right-hand men cannot kill Hanzo directly, so they send their fencing instructor, Junai Mikoshiba, to duel Hanzo. Lord Okubo changes his mind and calls off Junai. And so, Hanzo becomes a marked man.

When Hanzo searches the thieves, he finds women's clothing. They claim they stripped them off a dead body at the watermill hut. Upon finding the body, Hanzo suspects she was a rich merchant's daughter, but fingers her to determine that she had an abortion. So, they check the temples and shrines around for anyone who has performed the goma, a prayer to lose a baby.

We witness the goma, during which the witch performs an abortion in secret. Hanzo stops the ritual before the abortion can be started, letting the woman go. He interrogates the witch, bringing her the body of the dead woman, but the dead woman, Omachi Surugaya, left the convent alive. The film follows Hanzo as he follows the trail. It may just lead him straight to the top.

In <i><a href="https://boxd.it/1ney7X" rel="nofollow">Sword of Justice</a></i>, Hanzo used the powers of his massive penis, manipulation, and SA to solve crimes. Here, it's the exact same thing, but with such subject matter as rich merchants who pay nuns to torture and SA women in their care and training sequences where Hanzo beats his penis with a stick and fucks rice.

In other words, this is some exploitative Japanese nonsense. The nudity is rampant. The blood sprays everywhere. The score is all over the place — at one time funk, at another traditional — sometimes, it's mystical synths, and at other times, it's ominous noise.

The government has made it so that poor people cannot survive, so they have to get abortions. That very same government is authorizing the convents to sell women into coerced torture and SA to make that money. Hanzo uses the same tactics to interrogate multiple women. The movie doesn't question this, but I thought it was worth noting.

I thought maybe I had been too harsh on the first one, but no, this series kind of blows. The action is fine in the couple of places it happens, but if you want gore, it's primarily coming from murdering victims of assault. Otherwise, it's just SA. I wish I could remember why I downloaded these movies back in college. Perhaps someone who knows samurai films could remind me. Obviously, this type of movie has an audience. I am not one of them.
