If this is your first David Lowery, then I'm sorry if you weren't expecting this. So much of his work relies on words, and this movie is primarily spoken. The film primes its audience for an indistinguishable blend of the literal and the metaphorical before pulling us into an ethereal visual space. Like much of Lowery's work, however, the visuals are its greatest strength. The colors are rich and punchy. The dresses are phenomenal. The cinematography demands a large screen to take in the scale of every composition.

Thematically, the film explores reconciling pain and how it becomes art. Sam and Mary appear to have once been lovers and collaborators. But once fame gave Mary an adoring audience, she pushed Sam away. In that heartache we find something metaphysical—spooky action that binds the two beyond time and distance.

The performances are top-notch. I love the contrast between Michaela Coel's strength and Anna Hathaway's brokenness. Unfortunately, the two have zero chemistry, so I found it hard to believe they were ever in a relationship together, let alone one that would break them the way this movie conveys. Still, they held the movie together. Also, I am willing to blame the sexless nature of the filmmaking for the chemistry read.

Sam's last name, Anselm, references Anselm of Canterbury, who posited that Jesus redeemed humanity by performing the obedience humanity was incapable of. Mother Mary is a reference to... I'm kidding. Religious language permeates the movie, such as Sam calling her process the "transubstantiation of emotion into a dress." And, of course, the lyrics.

The music is the weakest link here. It feels like it’s going for Taylor Swift with religious hangups. Jack Antonoff is genuinely incapable of writing anything that sounds human or sincere. I wish I could've heard what Charli xcx and FKA twigs would do by themselves. Ending a musical phrase with the word "mouth" is so awkward, and this movie does it so much.

Still, I love many of the choices here. The lack of male speaking roles is refreshing, especially for a Lowery movie. I also appreciate the respectfulness around nudity — in the wrong hands, this movie could have been much more exploitative. This movie is very much not for everyone. But it cast a spell on me that I find hard to deny.
