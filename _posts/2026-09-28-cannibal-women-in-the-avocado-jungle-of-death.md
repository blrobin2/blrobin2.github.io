At Spritzer College, Dr. Margo Hunt gives a talk about historical cases where women in cultures have dominated men. Bunny approaches Dr. Hunt after class, voicing interest in switching majors to feminist studies. Dean Stockwell (as in the dean, not actor Dean Stockwell), along with Ford Maddox and Colonel Mattel of the National Security Commission: Avocado Affairs, approach Dr. Hunt.

Ford Maddox shows a map of the uncharted avocado jungles of Southern California, spanning from Bakersfield to the Mexican border, with the interior totally "wild and uncharted." Dr. Hunt confirms what the government officials suspect: that in that jungle live the Piranha women, an ancient tribe of radical feminists who eat their men. The military tried to take the Piranha women out, but failed.

So, the US government wants Dr. Hunt to convince the Piranha women to evacuate the jungle and move to a reservation in Malibu. Dr. Hunt only agrees because Dean Stockwell threatens to strip her department of funding if she doesn't. Dr. Hunt brings Bunny along to keep her from attending a wet T-shirt contest. Along the way, they hire a mercenary, a male chauvinist, and Dr. Hunt's one-time hookup, Jim.

J.F. Lawton (of <i>Pretty Woman</i> and <i>Under Siege</i> fame) wrote and directed this. As you can tell, this is a very silly movie. It has an 8-year-old's understanding of feminism (somewhere between "girls smart, boys stupid chauvinist pigs!" and "feminists are actually just afraid of men.").

That's not to say the movie doesn't have its funny moments, but they're few and come from absurd, Naked Gun or Looney Tunes-adjacent jokes, not observations on gender. The movie messes up most when it puts Bill Maher in it. Yes, he plays a misogynist buffoon who is the butt of the jokes. But he's also Bill Maher. And the movie lets him talk far too much.

Really, the movie is just boring. The movie has some production value, including a very beautiful jaguar. But it's simply incapable of surprising and takes the lazy joke every time.
