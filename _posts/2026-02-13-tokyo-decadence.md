Cold open to Ai bound to a complex S&M contraption, with a man promising not to hurt her. The man makes a distinction between the "whores" who sleep around in college only to marry rich and a sex worker like Ai, whom he worships. He straps a gag and blindfold to Ai, then pulls out a syringe and injects the now frightened Ai. She slips in and out, and the man removes her shoes and penetrates her with a dildo.

Ai sits in a park practicing some hand motions to a song she sings to herself. Her beeper goes off. She returns the call at a nearby payphone and receives instructions to arrive at The New Prince Hotel at 7 PM to meet with Mr. Ishioka. She kills time at the mall, grabbing a bite, playing some games in the arcade, and getting her fortune read. The fortune teller gives Ai three instructions:

1. Place a telephone directory under her TV.
2. Don't go to an art gallery in the east — a woman will get lost in a thick mist over there.
3. Find a pink stone, make a ring out of it, and wear it on her hand.

She goes to a store in the mall to get a pink stone. The jeweler recommends a pink Topaz. She has the ring made and puts it on before her appointment. Mr. Ishioka anoints her hair with oil, then tells her to stand and expose herself to the office windows. He makes her pull her panties down and up, over and over again, until he is satisfied. It takes hours, but finally he fingers her and forces her to yell that she's a horny businesswoman.

Before Ai did sex work, she did social work, but she now believes that she has no talent. But as we'll see, what keeps her stuck has more to do with an ex who left without saying goodbye.

<blockquote><i>I want to evolve. I want to live in a totally different world.</i></blockquote>

The film explores the post-bubble consumer market of Japan and the sexually frustrated businessmen who buy Ai's time for sexist rants and sadomasochism. Money is the last problem on their mind, and they throw it at Ai. Even the "nice" ones (maybe especially the nice ones) have the darkest fantasies. Losing control of their business lives, they turn to the oldest standby for ego: subjugating women.

<blockquote><i>My dream is to rape a dead woman. It's called necrophilia. It's the most beautiful form of sex in the world.</i></blockquote>

When Ai meets Saki, a dominatrix who deals exclusively with masochists, she sees how different Saki's life is from her own. But still, even with all the money she makes, she's still shooting up and snorting the same designer drugs as the businessmen. Still, unlike Ai, Saki isn't ruled by her past.

<blockquote><i>— You must be wealthy.<br>
— Not really. It's Japan that's wealthy. But it's wealth without pride. It creates anxiety, which drives our men into masochism. I've made my living out of these men.</i></blockquote>

I don't think Murakami knows how to translate his prose to a screenplay. He leaves all the space for the character's internal monologue, but none of the monologue, creating a super-relaxed pacing on par with Lynch's later work. It lets the S&M scenes play out in real time, and it gets sort of tedious after a while.

The filmmaking is solid — the cinematography is outstanding in places, creating the isolating and distorted loneliness of the city. The film smartly obscures some of its more explicit moments while still conveying some truly disturbing stuff. It still gets explicit with, um, bodily fluids.

The film aims to be "elevated pinku," but it doesn't do enough to justify the misery it portrays. Still, it finds a way to end the film that lifts the misery to a more human space. Don't miss the post-credit sequence.
