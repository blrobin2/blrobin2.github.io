<blockquote><i>Cry who can, laugh who </i>will — Chinese proverb</blockquote>

Roland drives through the port city of Nantes. A listless daydreamer, he longs to get away from the city. He arrives late to work because he wanted to finish a book. The boss calls him in, hears his excuses, and fires him.

Sailors wander through the city, stopping off at a cabaret to dance. One, Frankie, looks for Lola, with whom he is having an affair. They go back to her place to share some whiskey and cigarettes. Frankie reminds her of her first love, Michel.

At a bookstore, Roland meets Madame Desnoyers and her daughter, Cécile, who wants to learn English. Roland offers them his dictionary, saying that Cécile reminds him of a childhood friend of the same name.

Lola spots Roland across the way, and the two reconnect after fifteen years. Meanwhile, Roland accepts a suspicious job, unable to land anything else.

<blockquote><i>There's a bit of happiness in wanting happiness.</i></blockquote>

As director Jacques Demy's first directorial effort, he wanted to create a "musical without music." The film has a plot reminiscent of a musical, with simple relationships, talk of first loves, and plotlines that follow almost fairy-tale logic. At the midpoint of the movie, Lola practices her cabaret routine, performing what is essentially her theme song—it reads like a musical number, but it's diegetic.

Both Roland and Lola are obsessed with their first true loves. Both know that loving someone else is possible, but no love is as strong as their first. The classic love triangle emerges: Roland loves Lola, and Lola loves the missing Michel.

The film mirrors Lola's first love when Céline, Madame Desnoyers' daughter, meets Frankie and spends her birthday with him. As Lola's real name is also Céline and Frankie greatly resembles Michel, we experience that intensity of feeling, as well as young Céline's heartbreak in realizing that no love will feel like this one.

That isn't to say the film doesn't explore deeper experiences. For example, Lola wants one man to be a friend, but they all chase her, or at least their idea of her. By reconnecting with Roland, she hopes that, since they had met before she became a dancer, Roland could be a friend. But Roland has concocted some fantasy around Lola, and gets his heart broken when the feelings aren't mutual.

Demy continued to explore this line between grounded narrative and musical idealism, with movies like <i>Umbrellas of Cherbourg</i> and <a href="https://boxd.it/9GENbH" rel="nofollow"><i>The Young Girls of Rochefort</i></a>achieving their own distinct perfection of the style, and movies like <a href="https://boxd.it/6uF0Aj" rel="nofollow"><i>Donkey Skin</i></a>coming out on the other side in a fantastical world. So, if this approach doesn't work for you, perhaps his other films might. For a first film, though, it's quite an achievement.
