<b><a href="https://boxd.it/qaTwm/detail">Asian Cinema Challenge 2023 | 50/52 | Directed by Mohsen Makhmalbaf</a></b>

A former policeman looks for Mohsen Makhmalbaf. He catches Makhmalbaf's daughter outside their home and asks her to pass along a message. She can tell straight away that the man wants to be an actor. Twenty years ago, during the time of the Shah, the officer was stabbed, so he resigned. At that time, Makhmalbaf was a seventeen-year-old revolutionary. It was Makhmalbaf who, in trying to disarm the officer, stabbed him.

<blockquote><i>— What do you want to be?<br>
— I want to save mankind.<br>
— What does that mean?<br>
— I want to save poor people, oppressed people.<br>
— How will you do that?<br>
— Whichever way I can. I don't care how.</i></blockquote>

Cut to the casting process, where Makhmalbaf looks for an actor to portray him when he was seventeen. He asks each of them what they want to do with their lives. He chooses one who shares his reckless idealism. The police officer is also there, helping to cast the younger version of himself. He chooses the handsomest one who doesn't look like him at all. Mr. Zinal chooses a different actor who very closely resembles the officer and even shares many of his mannerisms, but the officer doesn't like the choice and leaves. What the officer really wants is to play the young version of himself.

<blockquote><i>— What is the film about?<br>
— I want to recapture my youth with a camera…</i></blockquote>

The officer returns, and they agree:  Makhmalbaf will direct the younger version of himself, and the officer will direct his younger counterpart. The officer gets the younger man measured for a police uniform. The officer says that, if the tailor does a good job with the suit, he will help the tailor get a part in the movie.

And so, the filmmaking process begins. But hasn't it already?

<blockquote><i>I don't understand. Are you an actor or a dreamer?</i></blockquote>

The film tells a true story: Mohsen Makhmalbaf stabbed a police officer at a protest rally. Twenty years later, they make amends by making this movie. Despite the documentary-like presentation, everything in the movie is a reenactment, including the casting process, but with the actual people. This blurring of fiction and reality is reminiscent of Kiarostami's <i><a href="https://boxd.it/9ikxoT">Close-Up</a></i>. If you've seen <i>Close-Up</i>, the main character, Hossain Sabzian, is posing as Mohsen Makhmalbaf.

Both films are part of the Second Iranian New Wave, which is often associated with postmodern approaches to storytelling. This film, however, differs from Kiarostami's in its approach. We watch Mirhadi Tayebi (the police officer) and Mohsen Makhmalbaf's differing approaches to preparing their younger selves to play them. Tayebi is concerned with the concrete: holding a gun, giving directions, carrying a flower. Makhmalbaf focuses on the young actor's personal loves and fixations, drawing them back to the role of Makhmalbaf.

The moments that the characters are recreating soon bleed into the story we are seeing. The young actors perform their parts as though no camera were present. The first time it happened felt like a fun moment of serendipity. When it happened again, mid-conversation, I cheered with delight.

Like <i>Close-Up</i>, it took the whole movie for me to see how I felt. I wish I could describe all of the intersecting layers of the movie, but that just means you need to see the film for yourself.

<blockquote><i>We must live, as long as there are trees.</i></blockquote>

#### Stray Thoughts/Spoilers

- The daughter character is so funny and incisive.
- I love the tailor's obsession with American movies, raving about Kirk Douglas and John Ford.
- The scene where the cousin's daughter brings soup to the young actor! They suddenly lock into their characters as though they were the characters and the moment was coming. It gave me such chills!
