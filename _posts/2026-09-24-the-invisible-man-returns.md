At Radcliffe Manor, the help eats their evening meal, discussing Sir Geoffrey Radcliffe's soon-to-be execution. The state believes Geoffrey killed his brother, Michael, but the people of the house know that he didn't commit the crime. Geoffrey's fiancée, Helen, and his cousin, Richard Cobb, wait in the parlor for any news. Dr. Frank Griffin, a close friend of Geoffrey's, visits with the prison governor, who has granted Griffin ten minutes with Geoffrey.

An hour or so passes, and the prison staff comes to collect Geoffrey, only to find his cell empty and his clothes piled on the floor. They call Scotland Yard to speak with Detective Sampson regarding the escape. An invisible form wanders through the woods and comes across a flag tied to a tree. Beneath, in the brush, is a suitcase with a change of clothes. Cobb comes to Dr. Griffin's lab, ranting about Geoffrey's disappearance, and now Helen's, but Griffin has no news.

At Radcliffe Collieries, Willie Spears has taken over leadership, though he struggles to manage the men with the news of Geoffrey's escape. Detective Sampson arrives to interview Dr. Griffin. They've read the file on Griffin's late brother, John, who had developed a serum capable of making the body transparent, but at the cost of driving the user insane. You know, the guy from the first movie.

Helen is hiding away at a cottage, telling the owner that Geoffrey has had a bad accident and is wrapped in bandages. The owner's dog barks as a figure approaches. Helen recognizes him as Geoffrey, despite his totally wrapped face. Geoffrey seems like himself in his humor, but the barking dog stirs a burst of anger that worries Helen. Geoffrey is also hellbent on finding Michael's true murderer.

Will Dr. Griffin find the antidote in time before the invisibility drives him mad, just like John? Will Geoffrey find Michael's true murderer? Will Detective Sampson solve the case or will he believe, like the state, that Geoffrey is guilty? What's Willie Spears' deal? Or Richard Cobb's?

Boy, this movie sure has a lot of plot!

The film explores human interiority and how, despite our best efforts, we cannot know what lives in a person's mind. Our concept of each other is a useful fiction with doses of reality mixed in. On the other hand, people are prone to revealing aspects of themselves they believe they keep well-hidden—an ill-timed joke, a knowing look, or an unexpected emotional response.

In other words, this film retreads the familiar territory of the first movie. The film doesn't do much with the invisibility concept, though we do see a cute invisible guinea pig reappear. Vincent Price does an appropriate amount of scene-chewing to help lift this out of the middle of the road.

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- This film is ostensibly Vincent Price's first horror film, although 1939's <i>Tower of London</i> is close enough to horror in my estimation.
- This movie came off the success of <i><a href="https://boxd.it/6duTUX">Son of Frankenstein</a></i>, which, like this movie, sets the whole thing up without needing any of the original cast.
- The main reason this movie has so many "comedic" moments is top capitalize on <i><a href="https://boxd.it/3XR7ZL">Topper</a></i>'s success.
- Gorgeous sets with gothic undertones, especially Radcliffe Manor. The foggy cottage is also quite nice.
- Dr. Griffin makes a knowing joke when Cobb worries that the police will shoot Geoffrey "on sight." The movie later revisits this joke in a more blunt fashion.
- The invisible guinea in the experiment is so funny. And when it reappears, it's so cute! And when it dies, it's too sad :(
- When Willie Spears hears Geoffrey talking, he thinks it's satan. I guess because he's drunk. That scene has a lot of silly and fun camera work.
- Vincent Price does the Thriller laugh!
- The antidote, it turns out, is a blood donation.
