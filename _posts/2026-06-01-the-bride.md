<b>Joker for BookTok — Barbie for Hot Topic girlies</b>

In the original <i><a href="https://boxd.it/3p9E45">Bride of Frankenstein</a></i>, the titular Bride is barely in the movie, only (spoiler alert) coming alive in the final minutes to look upon Frankenstein's Monster in horror. In this film, Mary Shelley possesses a sex worker who was already marked for death and centers the Bride in the narrative. And yet, do we ever get to know her?

I don't mind most of the hammy pastiche and homage — Bartleby the Scrviner's "I would prefer not to," Ginger Rogers, Myrna (mal)Loy, the <a href="https://boxd.it/2dfaeP">dance routine to "Puttin' on the Ritz,"</a> etc, etc. The Bonnie and Clyde angle, mixed with the "Riot grrrl" (yes, a headline calls them that) copycat killers, is a bit tired, leading to a depleting, self-defeating finale. Of course, "me too" becomes a central piece of the climax.

The film's biggest misstep is taking pull quotes and protests from the police murdering black people and remapping them onto white women. "I can't breathe" and "Say her name" are refrains. I would accept an argument that they aren't the author's intentions, but I think that blindness to what the film is invoking isn't a good look either.

I still love Jessie Buckley despite a career of misguided pseudo-feminist films (<i>Men</i>, <i><a href="https://boxd.it/3IdTsP">Women Talking</a></i>, <i><a href="https://boxd.it/6dXrDn">Wicked Little Letters</a></i>). She fully commits to the role and keeps the film from ever getting boring. Christian Bale has felt like he lives on another planet for years now, but his Monster is the ideal surrogate for well-meaning men who still turn women into objects and cures for the loneliness that only they themselves can fulfill. Poor Penelope Cruz — it seems her character breakdown was, "She's a detective, but a GIRL?!" If that's the trope you're playing on, at least give her a background so that it doesn't become her only defining feature, thereby reinforcing the very cliché you are referencing?

I was never bored, but I was frustrated with so much to appreciate at the start, and so much of it squandered by the end.
