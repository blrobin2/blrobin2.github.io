Nolan's take on the material was never going to floor me — he long ago traded away any inclination toward poetry in favor of spectacle. In his adaptations, we see just how much of himself he sees in his leading men, even going so far as to give one of the biggest assholes in mythology a redemption arc.

But the sensory onslaught of IMAX obscures many of the character incongruities, (a couple of) paper-thin performances, and flattening literalism. It feels big and epic, and the final sequence of the movie is this year's stand up and cheer moment. See it in theaters unless you have a home theater, because the sound mixing will drive you insane unless you have no neighbors in a 2+ mile radius.

Look, it's quite good! I'm just not the target audience.
