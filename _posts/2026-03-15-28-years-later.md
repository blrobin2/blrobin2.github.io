I'm fine with movies making swings. I don't care about the jumpy edits or the stock footage that was only in five minutes of the movie. I'm ambivalent about the Young Fathers doing their TV on the Radio-esque punk, whatever. Be earnest! Wear your heart on your sleeve. But know that if you bury your sincerity under functionally empty stylistic flourishes, I'm going to have a harder time connecting to your movie.

If I had the Jody Comer disease in this movie, where I could forget what happened 20 minutes prior, I could appreciate the reflection on the frailty of life buried under the pile of bones and boners. The total inconsistency of this movie from start to finish only reinforces the feeling that I just watched the first 2-3 episodes of a longer TV show.

Thinking back on another Alex Garland project, <a href="https://boxd.it/6f4xhb"><i>Civil War</i></a>, I realized that this movie is sort of the inverse of that one. <i>Civil War</i> was a stylish, composed vision that had nothing to say. This movie has themes, but stuffing them inside a <i>28 \_\_ Later</i> carcass and dosing it with epilepsy does it no favors. Like, this movie is technically complete on its own, but it contains so many fragments — if I didn't know this was the first of three movies, I would have hated how it ended.

<i>[Obama voice]</i> Now let me be clear <i>[end Obama voice] [regret Obama voice]</i> — I'm genuinely happy for people who loved this and found it moving. I'm glad it exists! It just did nothing for me. Here's to hoping <i>Bone Temple</i> has something other than tricks up its sleeves.
