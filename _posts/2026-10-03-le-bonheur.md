<b><a href="https://boxd.it/q5w9E/detail">Criterion Challenge 2021 | 46/52 | Directed by Agnes Varda</a></b>

Birdsong. Sunflower in rich technicolor. In the background, the Chevalier family walks hand in hand: father François, mother Thérèse, and their two toddlers, daughter Gisou and son Pierrot. Cut to them after a picnic, where Thérèse pours water over the fire before lying down beside François underneath a tree. The couple kiss and Thérèse wishes François a Happy Father's Day. François asks the kids to compliment their mother's dress.

Later, the family picks flowers and delights in the serenity of nature before packing up and heading back to the car. Joseph's aunt, Madeleine, and the family meet up with François' uncle, Joseph, and enter the house where Madeleine continues to watch the movie. That night, Thérèse and François tuck in their children, then lie in bed and reminisce about the beautiful day.

The next day, François goes to work as a joiner for Joseph. A bride-to-be and her mother come to Thérèse for a wedding dress, which Thérèse makes by hand. While working in Vincennes, François stops by the post office to make a phone call. A post office worker named Émilie sets up the call. When François says the call's location, Fontenay, Émilie mentions that she'll be moving there in the summer.

The film follows the Chevalier family as François spends more and more time with Émilie, unbeknownst to Thérèse, who only sees a husband happier than he has been in a long time.

<blockquote><i>— My name is Émilie Savignac.<br>
— Émilie, I love you. I mean, I'm happy to know you.</i></blockquote>

The film never lets up on the cheery technicolor and delighted score, treating the forming affair as just another facet of the Chevalier family's happy life. The cuts are sometimes rapid and frantic, as though the camera cannot decide what to focus on. It's the most "French New Wave" aspect of the movie.

After François meets Émilie, the movie starts to use fades-to-red to transition between scenes, as though a fire is developing behind the film. The film also utilizes fades to blue, suggesting the separate threads of François' life. Soon, the two start flashing against one another as these two threads threaten to intersect.

The film features real-life couple Jean-Claude and Claire Drouot as François and Thérèse. Their actual children play Gisou and Pierrot. This adds an extra layer of anxiety and discomfort as the movie progresses, heightening the sense of realism.

Throughout the movie, ideas about how women "ought" to be around men surface. Joseph's aunt, Madeleine, watches a movie on TV that depicts two lovers beneath a tree. The woman is infatuated with the man's every word as he mansplains evolution, and she acts too stupid to understand but is enamored all the same. When François returns home from work, Thérèse gives him a kind greeting and the bride's mother tells her to take note of how she ought to talk to a husband.

<blockquote><i>You're wonderful, always doing what I hope you'll do.</i></blockquote>

François and the boys in the movie are fixated on Westerns: the notion of the individual man who protects women from Indians. The man who can take what he wants, so long as it pleases him. Because who will stop him?  Émilie feeds into that idea, offering him the freedom to come and go as he pleases. The men at François' work talk about going to dances, and one of the younger men joins in:

<blockquote><i>— I can sing duets as long as I change partners often.<br>
— I'd rather have just one good partner, says the young man.<br>
— Because you haven't had one yet! Later you'll want them all.</i></blockquote>

Agnes Varda knew it takes some women their whole lives to realize that men only ever make a woman's life harder. Whatever comfort or stability they bring is more a consequence of patriarchal gender hierarchies than any intrinsic value. This film is a nightmare wrapped in the skin of an idyllic romance.

<blockquote><i>— Kiss me if you won't give me coffee.<br>
— You want a kiss or coffee?<br>
— Everything.</i></blockquote>
