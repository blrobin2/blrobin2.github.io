Holy Moses, dressed like Judas Priest and sounding like Huey Lewis, performs for an excited audience of predominantly women. Backstage, their manager Ron tells the band that he needs photos of them covered in groupies to impress record exec, Don Matson. One girl, Cassie, sits away from the others. Jessie, the lead singer and bassist, asks her what she wants. She brings up their show at Grand Guignol the following night and tells him not to go.

The next day, while Ron drives the band to their next gig, Jessie works on some lyrics he pulled from a medieval grimoire. The book claims that the words can raise the dead. The van picks up Elsa, a woman we see kill two guys in the opening. She takes them to her weird house, which includes Elsa's werewolf mother, Eva, howling and swinging around knives. The band goes into town and acts like they're performing for a music video. The police arrest the band for some reason and cancel the performance.

Will the band perform? Or will someone kill them? Why not both?

The movie is exploitative in unusual, but not surprising ways. Like, the groupies raiding the band's dressing room backstage non-consensually while they're dressing. It gets so gross when Ron says, "Jessie, sign those nubile, underage tits, man." Jessie preys on Cassie, who is clearly a teenager. I understand, that's how rock music was at the time — doesn't mean I have to like it.

And to make matters weirder, Elsa's mother, Eva, is Eva Braun, and her father is Adolf Hitler, 95 years old and having sex like a champ, apparently. The movie seems to be going for an edgelord Addams Family thing.

The film tries to be funny and satirical, but it doesn't really have a target to satirize, so it's just sort of random. Because it has so many spinning plates — a killer supernatural family, Hitler trying to get a Fourth Reich going, a medieval zombie grimoire, a backward town banning rock n roll, etc, etc — it just doesn't do anything particularly well. And it totally falls apart in the 3rd act.

Here's what I like about the movie: the songs are bops! I guess I like that the murder victims are predominantly men. Some of the gore scenes are pretty effective — the zombie eating his own face is silly, but it works. The movie ties it all together in the final five minutes, but the journey isn't worth it.
