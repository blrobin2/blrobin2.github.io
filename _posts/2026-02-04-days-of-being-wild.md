<b><a href="https://boxd.it/qaTwm">Asian Cinema Challenge 2023 | 41/52 | Directed by Wong Kar-wai</a></b>

<b>April 1960, Hong Kong</b>

<blockquote><i>I want you to hate me. At least that way you won't forget me.</i></blockquote>

Yude grabs a bottle of Coke from the cooler. Su Li-Zhen takes his payment at the register. He asks for her name. She denies him, but he already knows. He tells her she'll see him in her dreams and leaves. He continues to drop by day after day to flirt, and Li-Zhen slowly finds herself drawn to him.

The two hook up. Afterward, Li-Zhen confides in Yude that her sister is moving out to live with her husband, leaving Li-Zhen in need of a place. She wants to live with Yude. He agrees. When Li-Zhen asks him, indirectly, what they should call their relationship, Yude makes it clear he's not interested in anything more than a hook-up. Crestfallen, she leaves, intending never to return.

Yude beats up a guy who stole his mother, Rebecca's, earrings. He passes Mimi, who tries to steal the earrings herself. So, Yude uses the earrings to lure Lulu back to his place. She tries to leave, and he pins her down, slowly forcing her to kiss him. They go to the bedroom and hook up. Lulu gives Yude her number and her real name, Leung Fung-ying. He says maybe he'll call.

The film follows Yude and the people he leaves in his wake as they each find something to cling to — something once theirs, but no longer. For Yude, it's the name of his birth mother, which Rebecca refuses to give him.

<blockquote><i>Don't tell others you're unhappy. Work harder. Go home and get to bed early. </i></blockquote>

The saturated greens tell us that Christopher Doyle is shooting this, his first collaboration of seven with Wong Kar-Wai. This film is also the first of three movies Wong Kar-Wai made around the theme of longing (the other two being <i><a href="https://boxd.it/aec49L">In the Mood for Love</a></i> and <a href="https://boxd.it/4VcBvf"><i>2046</i></a>). As an earlier film by Wong Kar-Wai, it lacks some of the stylistic flourishes that his later films would develop. But it does contain his signature interlocking storytelling, where we follow characters at different points in time throughout.

The title, taken from the Cantonese title given to <i>Rebel Without a Cause</i>, hints at this film's similar concern with moral decay. More directly, it creates a comparison between Jim Stark (James Dean) and Yude. Both face complicated home lives that drive them to be reckless. Wild, you might say.

Rebecca is a former sex worker who seems to operate on a tit-for-tat approach to every relationship in her life. She's with a man who only wants her for her money. But he makes her happy, so it's worth it. Yude wants his mother's name, but Rebecca gets nothing out of the exchange — if she had told Yude when he was younger, he would've run away. If she told him now, he would find his mother and forget Rebecca.

We see how this form of love infected Yude's ability to relate to people around him. Li-Zhen was a hook-up. Lulu is content to be strung along. Yude's best friend, Zeb, barely sees him and gets the cold shoulder whenever he tries—the pain of being unwanted cascades outwards.

While this isn't the most striking or stylish Wong Kar-Wai film, it still has plenty to appreciate in its melodrama and characters.
