<b><a href="https://boxd.it/q5w9E" rel="nofollow">Criterion Challenge 2021 | 18/52 | 2000s</a></b>

Mark sits outside his Portland home, meditating after striking a meditation bowl. Inside, his pregnant wife, Tanya, blends a green smoothie. Kurt calls — he wants to do an overnight trip, checking out some hot springs near Estacada, camp out in the woods, and hike back the next day. Mark runs it by Tanya, who is annoyed that Mark is pretending like she has a say. So, Mark packs up the car and brings their dog, Lucy, and hits the road.

When Mark arrives at Kurt's, he knocks but gets no answer. Without an option, he sits on the porch and waits. Kurt arrives with a toy wagon, carting a TV. He had an amazing time on his retreat in Ashland. They load up in Mark's car, picking up a baggie of weed and listening to political radio for a bit before drifting into conversations about Mark's dad. Kurt has some records he wants to take down to Sid's to pawn, but Sid's has closed due to increased rent and is moving to eBay.

They stop for gas, and Kurt takes Lucy to some grass to relieve herself. They grab some ice and groceries to throw in the cooler. Mark throws Kurt a koozie, but Kurt is disappointed that they're not funny. Kurt talks about possibly working for a restaurant and writing their menu. He talks about dancing with their old friend Yogi and having a magical night. Kurt sends them down a dirt road, and Mark reluctantly agrees to follow his lead. They back up, turn around, drive over the same bridge repeatedly, only to realize they are lost.

The film follows Mark, Kurt, and Lucy as they figure out how to move forward.

<blockquote><i>There's trees in the city and garbage in the forest. What's the big difference, you know?</i></blockquote>

I've known a few Kurts in my life. People who think they have the whole world figured out, lying to everyone, including themselves. Mark is the people pleaser, trying to engage with Kurt's nonsense politely, but incredulity has a way of seeping out. We catch the indirectness in the conversations. The passive-aggression, avoidance, and resentment. The performance of friendliness for someone you've lost touch with.

The film starts with a meditation and never really leaves it. By connecting to Mark's sit, we hear the sound of kids, birdsong, and wind through the trees. The movie takes time to watch the landscape grow ever more rural through the car windows while Yo La Tengo rides one of their signature mellow grooves. The film carries so much of the personality that Reichardt established 12 years earlier with <a href="https://boxd.it/4GZaqv" title="River of Grass"><i>River of Grass</i></a>. The difference, however, is the confidence that this film carries in its silence.

The performances are so perfect. Daniel London does so much listening and reflecting, and we get to watch it all. Will Oldham has known some Kurts in his life, too, because he nails that balance between shaky confidence and dedication to the lie. And Lucy the Dog is, of course, immaculate. I wish she had gotten to keep that big stick!

Mark listens to a political debate radio show in the car, both of which cover the loss of Democratic power in America. The first segment is about Lyndon B. Johnson's involvement in the Southern Strategy. The second is the classic tale of Democrats sitting on their hands rather than connecting with the people. When Mark was a hippie, it was during the optimism of the 90s, when folks really believed things were changing for the better (ask any minority, and they'll tell you just how status quo the 90s were). Mark has changed with the times and domesticated, while Kurt has kept to the lifestyle, drifting further into his own atmosphere.

<blockquote><i>It's okay — you're okay — sorrow is nothing but worn-out joy.</i></blockquote>
