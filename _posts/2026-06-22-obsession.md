This film crawled into my skin because I saw versions of myself in both Bear and Nikki. The whole thing works because Bear is such a "nice guy" and a coward — he made a choice that irrevocably messes up Nikki's life, and the lives of those around him. Because he thinks so little of himself, he tries to make it work. Nikki, post-wish, is rendered so codependent that she cannot function without Bear around.

And I have been both of those people — obviously not to this extreme, and not these days, but enough that I felt a little shell-shocked after. Also, the film has a gnarly scene that I sort of wished I looked away from.

The music is brilliant, painting beautiful, almost romantic synths, only to let them melt and distort. Or, when something funny happens, it cuts out like someone tripping over the aux.

The lighting and cinematography also really sell the whole experience, creating authentic jump scares and unnerving visuals at the periphery that keep the audience on edge.

The performances are solid, but Inde Navarrette has the hardest job, being someone who is both infatuated and trapped, both aspects fighting for center stage. Michael Johnston is good too — I think Curry Barker's strength is in directing their performances and pulling at all the contradictions at work.

Funny, horrifying, frantic, and infuriating — I'm so glad I saw this one in the theater, because I don't think it would have had the same impact without the audience and my full attention.
