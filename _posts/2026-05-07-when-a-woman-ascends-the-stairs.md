<b><a href="https://boxd.it/q5w9E">Criterion Challenge 2021 | 14/52 | 1960s</a></b>

<blockquote><i>Women working in the Ginza fought desperately for survival. It was a battle I couldn't afford to lose.</i></blockquote>

At Lilac Bar in Tokyo's Ginza district, the hostesses hold an engagement party for Miyuki and her new husband, Mr. Matsui. Keiko is running late, as the owner is chewing her out and her manager, Mr. Komatsu, for her dwindling numbers. Old standbys like Minobe are going to Yuri's bar these days. Yuri was once a protoge of Keiko. The owner wants Keiko to call and beg him to come back. As they leave, Mr. Komatsu encourages Keiko to open her own bar.

As night approaches, their work begins. Keiko gets her hair done in a more matronly, old-fashioned style. Meanwhile, the other girls don Western-style makeup and clothes—someone posing as Keiko calls Minobe, begging him to visit, surprising Keiko when she arrives. Minobe complains that the space is dead and routine. He offers to take Keiko to Yuri's bar to see for himself. While there, Yuri hints that things aren't going as well for her as appearances suggest.

Keiko decides to jump ship from the sinking Lilac and starts working at Bar Carton. Several of her regulars follow her to the new place. The owner had to pay off the tabs of Keiko's regulars at Lilac, so Keiko must raise the money to pay off her debt. She sends Mr. Komatsu around the following day to collect on the debts. Meanwhile, her mother calls, demanding that she send them money.

The film follows Keiko as she attempts to survive the grind of everyday life.

<blockquote><i>I had a dream where I was crying. When I woke up, I was really crying.</i></blockquote>

The film explores the impossible predicament of being a woman approaching 30, especially those in customer-facing jobs. Hostesses must have nice apartments, expensive clothes, and perfume — accessories that reflect a lavish lifestyle they cannot afford. Most of Keiko's fellow hostesses hope to marry one of their patrons and escape the cycle. Some, however, have ambitions to start their own bar. But as we see with Yuri, even when all appearances point to success, the debts accrue, and the difficulties multiply.

The stairs in the title are literal and figurative. In the literal sense, they are the same stairs Keiko walks up each evening to the bar where she works. Figuratively, the stairs are the small successes Keiko attempts while maintaining her dignity and moral code. All the money moving around is someone else's, whether it comes from wealthy patrons or broke hostesses. All the debts Keiko owes will go to the bar's owner. Add to this Keiko's brother and mother, who misunderstand Keiko's life, believing she has plenty of money to spend on their needs and get them out of trouble. And so, with each step Keiko takes, the remaining steps to success only grow.

In both <i><a href="https://boxd.it/dgnXtZ">Yearning</a></i> and this film, Hideko Takamine plays a widow who holds traditional Japanese values in a world that challenges them. Keiko says she will never let herself fall for any of her customers or marry them, because she is afraid of what will happen when she lets that guard down. In large part, this is due to her dead husband, whom she does not want to dishonor. Also, in Keiko's mind, a hostess who goes home with a customer is worse off than all.

But life happens — no one is all good or all evil. Humans are full of contradictions that life may never rectify. Kindness and self-preservation aren't always mutually exclusive, but both can make you an unwitting victim. If you can endure the pain of lost opportunities and former lovers, you may find a way forward.
