<b>San Francisco, The Day After Tomorrow</b>

Diane walks through the bustling city. As she jaywalks a side street, a man in a car collides with her. The front wheel falls off the vehicle. Diane is smiling, unbothered. Craig Gamble sits down to a cheese sandwich with his date, Louise, who is less than amused. As Louise runs out, Diane enters. Diane sits to eat the sandwich, calling Craig "Todd Armstrong," but he assures her that he is SIC agent Craig Gamble (code name 00½). Still, her charms win Craig over, and he takes her back to his place.

Back at Craig's place, Diane removes her trenchcoat to reveal a golden swimsuit. From Dr. Goldfoot's secret lair, Goldfoot's assistant Igor watches bashfully. When Goldfoot enters, he chastises Igor for sending Diane to the wrong target, and a broke target at that. Dr. Goldfoot sends a reprogram to Diane. She turns face, pushing Craig away in disgust and storms out. They send Diane to Todd Armstrong, the nation's wealthiest and most elusive bachelor.

Todd Armstrong's car suddenly goes in reverse. He spots Diane (or Number Eleven, as Goldfoot calls her), and wrecks his car in excitement. Still, Diane asks for a ride. Meanwhile, Goldfoot programs his other ten gold-bikini-clad robots on their respective rich targets. Instead, they start dancing, frustrating Goldfoot. Craig calls the operator, looking for Diane.

Will Craig walk right back into trouble? What is to happen to Todd? What will Goldfoot do with all his targeted rich bachelors?

The movie is a weird blend of Eurospy and beach movie, with Frankie Avalon playing a prat-falling, lovesick 00 agent, and Vincent Price playing the mad scientist (Goldfoot, an obvious play on <a href="https://boxd.it/5ogayZ"><i>Goldfinger</i></a>).

The movie is so silly — the sense of humor reminds me of Bewitched, Gilligan's Island, and other TV of the time. It's so frivolous and never dares take itself seriously, so it's hard to hate on it. Also, Vincent Price is so campy and ridiculous — all my positive points go to him.

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- The Art Clokey claymation title sequence is a blast — robots turning men into gold and melting them.
- The movie was originally a musical, and Vincent Price sang a song about the bikini machine, but producer Samuel Z. Arkoff thought the number made Price look too "fey." It's a beach party movie, though, so there is still band a performance and dances to break up the film.
- Dwayne Hickman, who plays Todd Armstrong, was deeply embarrassed by his drunk performance in the movie, calling it his worst he'd ever played.
- I love the hallway of paintings of Dr. Goldfoot's relatives — they're all Vincent Price in different genre outfits.
- We get a reference to <i><a href="https://boxd.it/4E0qXb">The Pit and the Pendulum</a></i> when Goldfoot straps Todd to the device.
- Susan Hart has to speak in so many languages, and she butchers every one of them.
