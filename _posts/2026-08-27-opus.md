<blockquote><i>A little constructive criticism never killed anyone.</i></blockquote>

We've seen the movie before: an eccentric genius lives apart from the world, inviting guests into their twisted games for some purpose, whether it's ritual sacrifice, a science experiment, or personal grudges.

John Malkovich gets the opportunity to be as absurd and over-the-top as he can manage. He monologues over at least 2/3 of the movie, and none of it coheres into a singular idea. He gets one more monologue near the end that feels like it’s meant to paper over the inconsistencies, but it just doesn't fit with the experience we had up to that point.

I wish we got to know a little more about the Level cult! It really hits this "teach them young" thing from the book, but it never comes back around. Like, one kid accidentally shoots someone with an arrow, but that's it.

The movie makes the rookie mistake of showing/playing the supposedly genius artwork. And some of it is so bad that it feels like we should be laughing at it, but the characters all believe that it is brilliant art.

Aesthetically, the movie works — the colors are theoried out the yin-yang, the cinematography is full of A24 tropes, and the score hits the right beats. The gore is effective, although it is incredibly indulgent when focusing on and repeatedly cutting back to the damage done to the Asian-American character.

So, this movie didn't do much for me. Still, I'm glad I saw it after the hate train died down. I can't say I loved it, but I understand its appeal. I'll still check out Mark Anthony Green's next movie if he gets the chance to make it.
