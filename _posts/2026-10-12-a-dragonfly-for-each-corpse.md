<b><a href="https://boxd.it/qa26g/detail" rel="nofollow">Cult Movie Challenge 2019 | 50/52 | Paul Naschy</a></b>

A drug deal on Milan's streets. A black-gloved driver follows the buyer, Franco Perralti, as he gets in his car and heads to his studio. The driver enters the studio while Franco unwraps his heroin and belts up. The driver draws a sword, and chops up Franco, blood splattering the walls.

At the police station, detectives bring an older man who exposes himself to women into the office for interrogation. Inspector Paulo Scaporella joins them, threatening the exhibitionist with death. The police chief calls in Paulo with pictures of Franco's murder, citiing the similarities to the Guarnacelli case, including the presence of a blood-stained dragonfly left on the victim's body.

A sex worker named Lucia sweats in the bar as no men have approached her. Professor Sandro Campitelli watches her leave and follows. Lucia walks a dim path in Via Provaci. She turns a corner, and a person in a black trench coat extends a blade from their umbrella, stabbing her in the stomach. They leave behind a dragonfly.

The suits upstairs want Victorio to take on the dragonfly murder case, but the chief thinks Paulo could take the upgrade from perverts. Is this case Paulo's big break? Or will Paulo become another corpse adorned with a dragonfly?

The film explores the notion of justice. We first meet the police through a couple trying to report a missing child, but they can't help. What are police for, if not to protect citizens? Meanwhile, the killer is out killing ne'er-do-wells from the underworld: drug addicts, sex workers, pimps, and more. Paulo believes the killer is doing the police's job.

The first act is quite misleading—we get three murder scenes, then the movie gets much sparser. That isn't to say there aren't many more kill scenes. It's just not as relentless as the movie first suggests. Also, Paulo's fashion-designer wife, Silvana, is the best detective in the movie. Because it's giallo, Paulo has to call her clues "coincidences" so they don't solve the case too quickly.

I don't think Paul Naschy is ever in a shot without a cigar in his mouth. Even while cooking at home, the cigar just hangs there.
