<blockquote><i>As feudal Japan enjoys peace, the samurai era is waning. But this fragile calm is threatened by the growing power of Lord Naritsugu, the Shōgun's sadistic younger brother. Sir Doi realizes Naritsugu will ruin the Shogunate if he gains higher political standing. As Naritsugu's evil deeds are quickly hushed up, Sir Doi must act.</i></blockquote>

On March 5, 1844, Akashi Clan House Elder Zusho Mamiya commits harakiri as a protest to the Shōgun for his refusal to punish Lord Naritsugu. The Shogunate Council meets to determine its next course of action. Sir Doi approaches a Shōgun samurai, Shinzaemon Shimada, regarding Naritsugu. Shinzaemon does not believe Naritsugu is a worthy heir to the Akashi Clan and should step down. But the Shōgun has appointed Naritsugu as his senior advisor.

Shinzaemon speaks with feudal lord Yukie Makino of the Owari Clan in Kiso. Last April, Lord Naritsugu came through Kiso, so the clan enlisted their wives and daughters to provide full hospitality for him, as is the custom. Among them was Makino's daughter-in-law, Chise, whom Makino ordered to bring picture scrolls to Naritsugu. Naristugu promptly raped Chise. When her husband, Uneme, came looking for her, Naritsugu murdered Usume. Chise killed herself in shame. Makino wishes he could do the same.

If Sir Doi acts now, he will destroy the years of peace that the Shōgun's rule has brought. And so, he requests the help of Shinzaemon. To further convince him, Sir Doi shows him the daughter of the peasant leader, Naritsugu, who had her limbs cut off and her tongue cut out, dragged her around as a toy, then cast her out. The daughter uses her mouth to write out a message to Shinzaemon: TOTAL MASSACRE. Now that fate has given Shinzaemon the opportunity for a noble death, he agrees to assassinate Naritsugu.

The film follows this assassination plot — the hiring of the assassins, the spies who uncover it, and the struggle to restore order to Japan.

<blockquote><i>A time comes when servants rise up against their lords.</i></blockquote>

Miike's film is based on a 1960s film of the same name. Being a Miike film, this remake ups the sadism of its villain, showcasing his brutal rape, torture, and murder as often as it can. While the film depicts these horrifying acts of violence, Miike displays some restraint, leaving the worst details for the imagination. Still, the film is exploitative with women's bodies, showing their torture, murder, and mutilated bodies with no reservations.

One of the weirdest choices was intentionally hiring people with no fighting choreography experience. He didn't want it to look aesthetically pleasing, and it doesn't. Unfortunately, I could only find the dubbed version. The voice acting is on par with cheap import anime — I'm really doing my best not to judge the performances based on this terrible dubbing.

Thematically, the movie explores how Good must suffer incalculable loss in the war against Evil. Power structures are designed to uphold the status quo at any cost. To fight against them is a gamble that you must be willing to lose, if only to die knowing you fought for a just cause.

Critics compare this movie to Kurosawa. I guess because it borrows the same template as <i>Seven Samurai</i>. Also, it depicts many royals having conversations, drowning in decorum. Miike mentioned Kurosawa while making the film, saying he wanted to update Kurosawa for the next generation. I personally don't see anything beyond superficial comparisons.
