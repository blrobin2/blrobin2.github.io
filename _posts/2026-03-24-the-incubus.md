On November 1st, 2010, the entire town of Spoon River disappeared. All that remained was a diary written by resident Marnie Rose. In it, she laments her boring, unchanging life, while also complaining about how people waste the precious minutes of their lives. She lives with her religious and abusive uncle, Walter, and is friends with a punk/mod kid named Johnny Johnson (think Kyle Gallner in <i>Jennifer's Body</i>). At college, some bullies make fun of Marnie's weight and the fact that both her parents are dead.

We flashback to a day when a group of teenagers bedecked in 2000s goth-adjacent fashion enter the town. They enter the house of Mr. and Mrs. Jackson unannounced and claim it as their own. As Marnie drives herself and Johnny to school, they pass by and see the goth teenagers bring in a moving truck. The teenagers gather around one of them, dealing tarot cards, talking about developing deep bonds with the town, and about destroying the previous one they took quickly.

That night, Marnie and Johnny hit up a teen club with go-go dancers. Their friend Chris is going to perform there? One of the goth teens, named Raphael, prowls the edges of the room. He and Marnie lock eyes. She notices a faint glow in his eyes and passes out. The next day, we see Raphael stalk a woman while another one of his goth friends, Bianca, pursues a guy and sucks out his soul. Back home, Bianca tries to seduce Raphael, but his mind is on Marnie. At home, Walter beats Marnie for staying out late.

At school, a new transfer student joins the class. It's Raphael. What are his intentions with Marnie? Will he be her death or her redemption?

This project is quite ambitious! Shayne Leighton wrote, directed, starred, and recorded several songs for the soundtrack. The songs have an early Paramore/emo-pop sound. Marcie Gorman stepped in to help finance it and also took on a co-director position. Obviously, the film is a <i>Twilight</i> rip-off, with dashes of other teen movies from that era.

I'd call this a silver-star good-bad movie. It doesn't have the auteur vision of your <i>Room</i>s or <i>Fateful Finding</i>s, but it still has plenty of wacky choices that made me laugh.

EDIT:
On reflection, this movie IS an auteur vision. While Shayne Leighton doesn't have the name recognition of the directors of the movies mentioned above, she does have their singular drive to have it all. Also, she still had multiple small successes as a musician, actor, and author, which is far more than I can say. So sincere kudos to her!
