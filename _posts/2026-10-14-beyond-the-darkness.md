Francesco is a wealthy man who performs taxidermy on the side, from small birds to baboons. His housekeeper, Iris, works with a witch to curse Francesco's fiancée, Anna. Anna ends up in the hospital on life support. Anna begs Francesco to make love with her one last time before she dies. He kisses her, and she flatlines.

Back home, Francesco contemplates Anna's death, as well as the death of his parents, as he sits in the room that once belonged to them. Iris chastises him for being in his parents' room, but upon hearing of Anna's death, quietly celebrates being the only one who can care for him now. She opens her blouse and breastfeeds the 25-year-old man.

At the morgue, when he believes no one is around, Francesco injects Anna's body with the same preservative he used on the baboon. The mortician, however, sees Francesco work. The funeral proceeds, attended by Anna's parents and her twin sister, Teodora. That night, Francesco drives back to the cemetery and digs up Anna's body.

The film follows Francesco's obsession and the lengths he will go to keep it a secret. Meanwhile, the mortician starts his own investigation into Francesco's activities.

<blockquote><i>Body three times cursed. Pain and torture first. Body in a bed, body growing dead. Body in a crypt, body hellfire dipped. Body ringing bell, body into hell.</i></blockquote>

Though Joe D'Amato had a prolific career, he only made a handful of horror movies. With each, he hoped to push limits and disturb audiences. Here, D'Amato is at his most depraved, exploring necrophilia, cannibalism, and unrestrained gore. By using intestines, skin, and hearts from the butcher, the movie creates sufficiently realistic splatter. His goal was to make the audience throw up, and I'm sure he succeeded.

As a piece of filmmaking, this is probably D'Amato's most successful, at least in my experience. Its only goal seems to be revulsion. Therefore, I wouldn't recommend this to most audiences. However, I highly recommend Goblin's score — it rules.
