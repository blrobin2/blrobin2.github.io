The film explores the relationship between fable and reality. In this world, the mice believe a bear would eat them in a second, and bears believe mice are an infestation that destroys homes. Celestine the mouse is wary of the belief about bears, leading to an encounter with Ernest the bear. The two form a deep bond after committing crimes to help one another live.

The film plays like an allegory for immigrants, where each side believes the worst about the other. In both cases, the reality is that people are merely trying to survive, not ruin your culture or convert your family to their religion. In this way, the film also explores how laws prevent mere survival — ultimately, the crimes Ernest and Celestine commit together are to keep one another alive and safe.

What makes this movie truly beautiful is the time it spends with Ernest and Celestine while in hiding, where they learn about each other's artistic leanings and histories. They enable one another to be more expressive and to experience more of the world than they would if they were alone.

Yes, this is a movie for children. But it speaks to everyone. If I had a child, I would gleefully watch this movie with them over and over.
