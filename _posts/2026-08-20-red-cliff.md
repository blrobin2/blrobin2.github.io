<b><a href="https://boxd.it/qa26g/detail" rel="nofollow">Cult Movie Challenge 2019 | 32/52 | John Woo</a></b>

In 208 CE, during the Han Empire, South China leaders Liu Bei and Sun Quan rebel against the oppressive prime minister of the Imperial Army, Cao Cao. He appeals to Emperor Xian to allow him to lead the imperial army against them. So, the Southland leaders join forces to stop Cao Cao.

The film has an epic scale, with gorgeous costumes and outstanding sets. The film jumps to CGI for the biggest set pieces and difficult-to-coordinate moments at scale (rain of arrows), but still retains a huge cast to give the ground fights weight.

Woo's stylistic flourishes are a bit heavy-handed, as though he is terrified of a bored audience. We get the classics — indoor wind, too many candles, and white doves. The blood is over-the-top, especially the CGI spurts, but the fights also dive into wuxia territory, so a little exaggeration fits the tone. The film trades historical accuracy for cartoonish spectacle, with wild set pieces to break up the battles.

Tony Leung's save-the-cat moment is fixing a kid's slightly off-key flute. It's a pretty beautiful moment, even if its intentions are obvious. He's perhaps too old for this role, but I understand hiring him for his global recognition.

Woo has always been great at creating a sense of brotherhood. It’s a pleasure to watch men cheer each other on and encourage their strengths. The film also emphasizes Chinese ideals, such as unity and gender equality being keys to their heroes' success.

That said, the version I watched is a pacing nightmare. It had an English-speaking narrator explaining what's happening on screen. Over the 2.5-hour runtime, everything is happening all the time, with scenes sped up and chopped to pieces to fit all the essential story beats. If I cared a bit more, I'd seek out the 2-part version and get all the nuance and pacing that the filmmakers wanted.

THAT said, the film still works, especially during the quieter moments. It's just a bit too goofy and indulgent for my taste, especially for the genre.
