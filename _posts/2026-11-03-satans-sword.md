Omatsu and her grandfather hike up a steep mountain along the Great Buddha Pass. Omatsu hears water running and takes their water container to refill it. While she is gone, a shadow approaches her grandfather. Tricking him, the shadow draws his sword and strikes the man down. As the grandfather tumbles, his bell falls out of his hands and is caught on a tree branch. Omatsu returns too late. A man follows soon after and promises to take care of Omatsu.

Tsukue Ryunosuke trains for the Command Fencing Competition at Mt. Mitaki. A messenger informs him that his opponent in the upcoming contest will be Utsugi Bunnojo. Then Utsugi's sister, Ohama, visits Tsukue, begging him to forfeit and save Bunnojo from shame. Instead, Tsukue captures her and forces her to confess that she is Bunnojo's wife, not sister. Tsukue SAs her.

Later, a traveler passes Tsukue, and he knicks the traveler with his sword. The traveler, Omatsu's uncle, returns to her and promises to take her with him to Edo.  Tsukue learns that a thief has taken 300 ryo and his favorite Tantō. Ryunosuke's father tells him that someone with knowledge of secret techniques is striking down travelers. He also sees that Ryunosuke's skill has weakened in his arrogance.

On the day of the fight, Tsukue takes the "Silent" form, and both he and Utsugi stay still. The judge, Nakamura, calls a draw, but it is too late: Utsugi charges, and Tsukue cuts him down, killing him. Still, Nakamura maintains his ruling. In the wilderness, Ohama calls for Ryunosuke to warn him that several of Utsugi's students are planning an ambush. Utsugi divorced her, so she owes the family no loyalty.

Of course, Tsukue kills them all.  Ohama begs him to run away with her. Meanwhile, Utsugi Hyoma, Bunnojo's younger brother, rolls into town. Ryunosuke's father asks him to help teach Ryunosuke a lesson and give him what he deserves. He must watch out, because Ryunosuke has an evil sword. The film follows Hyoma as he trains for this fight, Omatsu as she tries to pull her life together, and Ryunosuke as he commits more evil deeds.

<blockquote><i>The sword is the soul; if the mind is evil, so is the sword!</i></blockquote>Like <a href="https://boxd.it/gUFMKb" title="Sleepy Eyes of Death"><i>Sleepy Eyes of Death</i></a>, this movie is heavy on the plot, perhaps even more so. It takes the unusual approach of following the villain for the first act, only introducing the hero after the villain has caused a lot of trouble. I've had a hard time winnowing down the details because we have three story threads going throughout. It's fun to see Raizō Ichikawa and Tamao Nakamura in another movie together.

This film was later remade as <i>The Sword of Doom</i> (or rather, both adapt the same source material). Though I haven't seen <i>The Sword of Doom</i>, it is highly regarded and in The Criterion Collection, so I assume it handles the material better. Director Kenji Misumi is best known for creating the <i>Lone Wolf and Cub</i> series, as well as directing several <i>Zatoichi</i>s. He did this movie before any of those.

I don't want to make it seem like this movie is bad. On the contrary, if you're into samurai flicks, you'll probably enjoy a fair bit about this one. The sets are beautiful, the cinematography is solid, and the fight choreography is grounded and thoughtful. Perhaps I'm just not enough of a fan of the genre to feel quite as strongly about this one. But I never had a bad time, even if I struggled to keep track of all the threads.
