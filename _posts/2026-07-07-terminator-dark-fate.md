<b>Livingston, Guatemala 1998</b>

Sarah and John Connor hide out, secure in the belief that they have stopped Judgment Day from coming. But they couldn't foresee another T-800 coming out of the woodwork and killing John.

<b>Mexico City 22 Years Later</b>

Grace, an augmented soldier from the future, arrives. Dani Ramos does some early morning shopping. Back home, she wakes up her brother, Diego, and feeds her father, Vincente. Dani and Diego leave the apartment for their job at an auto assembly plant. A Rev-9 model Terminator arrives, looking for "Danielle."

At the plant, Dani and Diego discover a new robot arm meant to replace some of the workers. Vincente appears, claiming to bring lunch for Dani. Grace takes security clothes and a gun. When Vincente sees Dani, he morphs the bagged lunch into a gun and tries to shoot Dani, but Grace saves her, shooting down the Rev-9 pretending to be Vincente, but not stopping him.

Grace has been sent back to protect Dani. Diego comes with them but dies in the ensuing chase. All hope seems lost until Sarah Connor arrives with the heavy artillery and gives them an advantage. Grace takes Dani and steals Sarah's truck, but nearly passes out due to limitations in her augmented metabolism. Sarah catches up and brings them to her hideout.

This ain't your daddy's Terminator! But it kind of is?

After <i><a href="https://boxd.it/f38Wdv">Terminator: Genisys</a></i> failed to ignite the reboot fever that Skynet, I mean Skydance, hoped for, Tim Miller asked James Cameron to help him make a sequel to <i><a href="https://boxd.it/eZyODV">T2</a></i>. Cameron, after seeing three financial disasters attached to his beloved movies, decided to help write. Again, they hoped to make a trilogy. Hubris! Besides bringing back Cameron, we also get Linda Hamilton reprising her role as Sarah, Arnold as the T-800, and Edward Furlong for some insulting motion-capture references.

Linda Hamilton is quite great in this — her trajectory across this series has been the best part of it. I wish they had just let her have her body as it was instead of forcing her through the debilitating athletic program. Mackenzie Davis is flat, but it's because her character is deeply underserved. Similar to <a href="https://boxd.it/ePvyHB"><i>T1</i></a>, Natalia Reyes gets a baby's breath of characterization before becoming simply the escort mission subject. The difference, however, is that, over the course of a few hours, she miraculously transforms from Sarah in <i>T1</i> to Sarah in <i>T2</i>, becoming the tactical leader that saves the day.

The film examines how privacy has been obliterated over the past decade. The Rev9 has no problem hunting Dani, even when they try to go off the grid, because it can access military drones, "community" cameras, and all the trade-offs we've made in the name of "security."

The film also explores the notion of the "good immigrant." The Rev-9's form resembles that of a Hispanic man. When he encounters different white people in positions of power, he uses the empty rhetoric that will appease them so he can get away with so much of what he does (serving in the military, prayer, and a pitch-perfect Texas accent). Dani, on the other hand, is merely a migrant trying to escape certain death. But that isn't enough for the US.

The biggest misfire the film makes is failing to explore the "one person to save them all" myth that drove <i>T2</i>. After all, when John dies at the beginning of this movie, the messiah trope just gets shifted to Dani. It's almost like many people could take the leadership helm, and the important part is the organized resistance. But that might accidentally make people feel empowered.

It does, however, get at fighting the common enemy rather than creating divisions along cultural borders. It would be very easy to read Legion as Capitalism — it's human-made and hasn't always existed, despite how many take it as a given in most of the world. It can be dismantled.

Perhaps this is the best sequel to <i>T2</i> we could get, but it's not something anyone was really asking for. It has some compelling ideas and bonkers action set pieces that read quite cleanly. But move some pieces around, and this isn't that much different from <i>T2</i>.

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- They literally recreate D-Day from WWII, but with the machines emerging from the water.
- It sucks when they clearly put so much effort into practical effects, only to paint over them with CGI, rendering the whole exercise pointless. The set pieces are big and stupid. The slow motion action shots are TOO MUCH.
- Sarah's "I'll be back" is silly.
- "You put 100 cops between you and a Terminator, you'll get 100 dead cops." Your point being, Grace?
- Sarah throws Dani's phone out the window because of the GPS, then tells Dani not to touch the chip bag, because it has Sarah's phone and the bag blocks the GPS.
- I need to get off the grid, man.
- It's satisfying to watch the Rev-9 mow down border patrol dipshits.
- I love the reveal of the T-800 Carl, but it's the most obvious reveal in the world.
- I also love the idea that, once a T-800 completes its objective, it is free to live a regular life. Of course, this one recognizes his mistake and tries to rectify it, but he does it while also being a husband and father.
- I like how the potato chip thing comes back, and that even the most diligent can become prey to surveillance.
- Arnold looks like the updated Quilted Northern mascot.
- "I won't be back." Nice.
- Draperies is the funniest choice for Carl's career. I love his rant about the type of drapes you put in a child's room.
- I like that Sarah has allies.
- This is such a DUDE movie — the Corona-adjacent beers, the close-up of grilling steaks, the gun porn.
- Oh man, the Hoover Dam set pieces are TOO MUCH. It feels like James Bond (negative).
- Why the fuck does the Rev-9 try to reason with the T-800? I guess part of the Rev-9 design is to integrate and charm.
- The T-800 always has to sacrifice himself in the end.
- It really hits us over the head with unearned moments of mourning for characters.
- Another sequel that ain't happening, dude.
