The SS Liberated Lady sits in the London docks on the Thames. Crew members load the boat up with stuff like guns and monster tranquilizers. Director Luce Habit is off to "darkest Africa" to make her film. Her assistant, Ima Goodbody, is worried about Luce's sadistic tendencies as a filmmaker. Luce goes on the hunt for her film's love interest: a soft, gentle man. So, she goes to the farmer's market, where men flock.

There, she witnesses blond-haired, blue-eyed, silver-toothed Ray Fay. Ray visits a poster shop and purchases an "original reproduction" of the classic King Kong poster (not pictured). The shopkeeper tries to upsell Ray, and Ray politely declines. In retaliation, Ray steps outside, pulls a table on himself, and claims to have a hurt leg, threatening to sue but settling on the poster.

Ray runs away, but gets caught by the shopkeeper. So Luce, who has watched it all unfold, pays for the poster and whatever damages Ray caused. At the bar, Luce buys Ray a beer, secretly poisoning it, causing Ray to faint. She bags him up and drags him on the boat. When he comes to, the boat is already in the ocean. Their destination? Lazanga, where they do the Konga.

The native guides chant "Kong." From here, the film follows the beats of King Kong, for the most part.

The movie does a gender-swapping thing, but it isn't really saying anything about gender — the gender tropes just swap, because a woman being mean is "sexy" and a man being effeminate is "funny."  All the women still dress in revealing outfits. In effect, the film reinforces gender essentialist notions about inherent gender qualities.

The subtitle, "SHE'S IN ONE OF HER MOODS AGAIN," tells you almost everything you need to know about the humor. I should also mention how weirdly racist the humor is — not just the "natives," but throwaway jokes about Chinese, Black, Jewish people, and more.

What sucks is I think the movie had good intentions. If so, this is one of the most thoroughly misguided movies I've seen.

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- Carol Drinkwater plays Ima Goodbody, two last names that are just two words put together.
- The boxes loaded onto the boat are labeled things like "More Stuff", "Etc", and "And".
- There's a "Queen Kong" theme song, performed twice in the movie, by a band calling themselves the Orangutangs. "Queen Kong is the chick with all the hair. Queen Kong, Queen Kong comes from I don't know where. Kong, kong, kong, kong, kong, kong, kong, kong, kong, kong. Queen, queen, queen, queen, queen, queen, queen, Queen Kong."
- Ray Fay, I suppose, is a play on Fay Wray, who played the damsel, Ann Darrow, in the original King Kong.
- Yikes, the "natives" are Black and Brown women in Carmen Miranda/Miss Chiquita attire, singing "Unga kunga bunga."
- The "natives" also do an Exorcist gag, where their heads spin and they spew green stuff.
- The gag with Queen Kong's thrown being a nice chair in front of a massive picnic table is so dumb that I laughed.
- When Ray gets kidnapped by the "natives," Luce worries for Ray because the islanders have not invented contraceptives yet.
- In this version, Queen Kong and Ray are mutually in love.
- The end of the movie makes this plea for women to have freedom of self-expression, making Queen Kong the mascot of the women's liberation movement. The women the segment focuses on are Playboy bunnies and sex workers.
- The song over the closing credits is a separate "Queen Kong" theme, more in the style of a ballad.
