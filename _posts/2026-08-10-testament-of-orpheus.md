The director finally plays himself and reflects on his body of work. The artist is always out of time, never belonging to one world — no one embodied that more than Cocteau, whose work spanned poetry, novels, plays, paintings, films, and more. Here, he pulls it all in, putting himself on trial and walking the same roads he has made his characters walk.

The resulting film is Cocteau's most cerebral, putting out unanswerable questions regarding philosophy and the nature of time and space. In one sense, it is self-indulgent to inject yourself into your work, but also, Cocteau's work has always been a method of self-reflection and scrutiny.

Like <a href="https://boxd.it/fJLemp"><i>Orpheus</i></a>, the film plays with familiar visual motifs — most notably, time reversal as erased drawings come back into existence and torn flowers return whole. The artist too dies repeatedly and comes back. The magic of film is to make the static and inert come to life over time.

Though Cocteau would make one more short film before his death, a mirror to his lost first short, this film was his last complete statement, feeling intentionally incomplete and elliptical.
