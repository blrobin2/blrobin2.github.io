Beth sees her dad, Charlie, hawking appliances on TV, but Peggy Sue isn't interested in her soon-to-be ex-husband's antics. She puts on a classic prom dress for the Buchanan High 25-year reunion. Pics of her and Charlie as the 1960 prom king and queen decorate the gymnasium. Peggy Sue runs into Walter, Carol, Richard, and his wife, Sharon. Everyone asks about "Crazy Charlie," but Peggy says he isn't coming.

On cue, Crazy Charlie appears, upsetting Peggy Sue. The reunion committee has selected a king and queen — Richard and Peggy Sue. Peggy Sue is in tears — as the cake approaches, she passes out. When she comes to, she's back in 1960 with all the memories of the past twenty-five years.

Peggy Sue always knew what she would do if she could go back. Now that she has, what will she do differently, knowing what she knows?

The vibes have the sentimentality of later-day Robert Zemeckis mixed with the high concept of every movie of Robert Zemeckis's career. I think this has more heart than <i>Back to the Future</i>, but a much less developed arc and structure.

This movie has so much potential and so many seeds for great ideas that it doesn't follow through on. The movie gets progressively messier as it tries to figure out what to do with Peggy Sue and Charlie, and by the end... well, I'll save it for the spoiler sections below.

Still, I like this movie! I don't know why — maybe I'm only liking what it could have been. Maybe, in its messiness, it did something successful that I can't pinpoint. Oh well!

#### What I Would Do Differently™️ / Spoilers

- Peggy Sue starts off developing an appreciation for her mother, as she has been a mother and can understand her now, but the movie throws that away after the first act. I think reconnecting with her family and figuring out what is most important to her would've helped give the story shape.
- She has a son, Scott, who she misses throughout the movie, but we never see him or hear what may have happened to him.
- Peggy Sue sees Charlie perform with a Black R&B group, and suddenly she realizes he contains multitudes and is willing to entertain him coming back into her life. Maybe make it something more substantial and NOT music-related since the music obsession is part of what pulled them apart.
- I wanted her to figure out that Michael, who she saw as mysterious and poetic, turns out to be a burnout loser—it would reinforce the idea of not knowing someone and how that can be romanticized or misunderstood.
- The multiple scenes dedicated to telling Richard how to invest in the future go nowhere.
- I love that she tells her grandparents about the time travel. I wanted Grandpa's Masonic lodge ritual to be what brought her back!
- The final scene is so messy. I can see how the filmmakers are trying to go for something more grounded and realistic, but it makes whatever themes the movie may have been going for obsolete. Like, what was the point of the experience? So she could realize that Charlie's okay? She's not the one that needed to change—Charlie, the cheater deadbeat, needed to change.

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- This movie ended up being a reunion of actors I've seen in many recent watches/rewatches.
- Marshall Crenshaw! I recognized his voice, and confirmed it when I saw him on stage at the reunion.
- Everyone at the reunion looks like young people in middle-aged makeup.
- Kathleen Turner is so delightful in this! It’s rare to get her to play such a sincere character.
- Nic Cage's choices are, unsurprisingly, weird. His nasal voice, fake teeth, and rooster hair were painful for his co-stars and nearly tanked the movie — apparently he was trying to channel Pokey from <i>The Gumby Show</i>.
- Jim Carrey is... Jim Carrey.
- Richard's burrito theory of time travel is... underdeveloped.
- "Peggy, you know what a penis is. Stay away from it!"
- Charlie sneaking into Peggy Sue's like dang ol Nosferatu. The wiggly fingers were so cartoonish.
- Is "Finger Poppin' Time" about fingering??
- So much good neon in this movie!
