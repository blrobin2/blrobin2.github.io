Kibagami Jubei, a mercenary ninja, retrieves a stolen sword from some thieves who intended to sell it back for 300 gold. In a storm, eight figures on horseback ride towards Shimoda village. The next day, all are dead except one — the surviving woman tells the neighboring village that a plague killed everyone in Shimoda, but it appears that someone is puppeting her.

Shimoda goes into quarantine, but the Chamberlain learns of the eight figures and sends his Koga team to investigate. The team quickly dies from supernatural powers, such as lightning and rock strength, leaving only Kagero alive. Tessai, the rock-skinned fighter, brings Kagero to a hut to SA, but Jubei is there and rescues her. Jubei and Kagero part ways. But when she reports back to the Chamberlain, he sends her back to investigate.

Tessai tracks down Jubei to finish the job. While they fight, Tessai's rock-hard skin starts to crumble. The distraction affords Jubei the advantage, and he uses Tessai's large blade against him, killing him. A mysterious older man named Dakuan congratulates Jubei and invites him to help take down the remaining members of the Eight Devils of Kimon. He also suggests that Kagero helped Jubei in that fight.

Who are these Eight Devils and what is their grand plan? Will Jubei, Kagero, and Dakuan have the skills necessary to bring down the Eight?

The film tries to blend spy fiction with historical drama, while simplifying both for a younger audience. This results in a ton of expository dialogue that makes my eyes glaze over, especially because it adds so little to the story. Like, there's a whole secret gold subplot that Jubei has been involved in since before the story began.

The animation is gorgeous. The best shots center the moon and use shadows and outlines to convey movement. The violence is over the top, with blood spraying everywhere. The supernatural powers compellingly heighten the fights. It makes sense why this was such a visual influence on <i>The Matrix</i>, and why the Wachowskis invited director Yoshiaki Kawajiri to contribute two segments to <i>The Animatrix</i>.

Like Wicked City, the women characters, no matter how capable, are exploited. When Tessai captures Kagero, his first act is to strip her down and SA her. The women Devils use some form of seduction in all of their fights. The Chamberlain has escorts that he is regularly having sex with. The film also just has a weird relationship to sex. Like, the fact that Gemma sleeps with both men and women is treated as an existential threat to the rest of the Devils.

I know that anime has so much variety to offer, but this sadistic, sexist shit seems to thrive in the US. I can appreciate the creativity here—the dude with a wasp's nest for a back is neat—but this one is deeply overrated and seems to stick around only because of what it influenced.
