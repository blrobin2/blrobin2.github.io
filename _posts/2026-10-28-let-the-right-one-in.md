Having recently watched <a href="https://boxd.it/gHy9qZ">the American remake</a>, I wanted to make sure that I wasn't looking back at it with rose-colored glasses. And, thank heavens, I was right. This version has a darkness and coldness that balance heart and fear in proper proportions. The pacing is immaculate. The lighting and color grading make everything visible, letting nothing hide.

I still remember how confusing this movie felt when I first watched it, because I was so unaccustomed to movies not telling you everything up front, which is probably why so many folks read it as a coming-of-age romance and not a horrifying tragedy in which an old vampire in a 12-year-old's body finds someone they can use.

To be fair, that is often the case with vampire movies. But I think, from a child's perspective, we see how consent and understanding get blurred. Oskar is 12, but he also fantasizes about killing people. Yes, in part, for revenge. But also because of the newspaper clippings he keeps, for something terrifying inside him.

Perhaps the most controversial scene is also one of the most misunderstood. The brief crotch shot is Oskar learning what Eli means when she says that she's not a girl. Yes, it may be because she is a vampire. But it is also because she was castrated and has no genitals. So, whatever bond she forms, it will never be sexual. And that was likely an intentional choice by Eli.

<a href="https://boxd.it/3jnXiJ">Smarter people than me have talked about the trans/queer reading of this movie</a>, so I will leave it to them to explicate. But this film offers plenty of room for conversation and reflection, which is why it is so easy to return to every time.
