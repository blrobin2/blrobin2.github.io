One Hawaiian night, a crowd watches a Hula dance. A criminal organization makes moves to take out political leaders — men marked with leis taken out by guns, flame-dancers, and crossbows. The senator vows to look into it, but is taken out as well.

The government looks for a man who can assemble a team to put a stop to these murders. They reluctantly settle on Drew Sevano — he's murdered everyone he's dealt with, but he's the best they've got. His job is to assemble seven assassins to take out the seven criminals at work.

Drew first goes to Alexa and her assistant, Jennie.  He assigns her The Hermit, whose one weakness is women — an area where Alexa excels. The movie steps through each assassin and their target. When they all meet, they discover they have, for whatever reason, a 30-minute window in which to take out all targets.

Andy Sedaris's take on <i>Seven Samurai</i> goes about how you would expect — crazy kills, corny dialogue, and women in bikinis. Unfortunately, this one lacks the pacing of his later movies, with a tedious middle section, and the humor leans more toward cheese (and racism) than absurdity. Still, it's silly enough to make for an enjoyable watch, especially in the action-packed third act.

Like, this is basically the formula for <i>Ocean's Eleven</i>, but with more naked people and explosions.
