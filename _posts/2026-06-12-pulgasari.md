Ami brings water to her father, the blacksmith Takse, and her partner, InDe, who is also a blacksmith. Takse discovers InDe is hiding armor and weapons and suspects that he is a rebel. Still, he asks InDe to marry Ami and take over the forge. One day, the governor enters town to ask that Takse make armor for his army, supplying ox-loads of iron from farming tools from other villages that the governor has wiped out. The governor's men raid the village for more iron, killing those who stand in their way. The rebels do their best to push back the soldiers, but the soldiers capture them.

Takse gathers the townspeople and returns the iron that the soldiers took for him. The governor learns of this and tortures Takse, who claims that Pulgasari came and ate the iron. They throw the broken and defeated Takse into a jail cell to die. The captured rebels go on hunger strike. Ami and her brother, Ana, go to help their father, but the guards hold her back. In his dying moments, Takse crafts a figure out of rice and mud resembling Pulgasari, asking him to save the people in a way that he could not. When the villagers carry his body away to be buried, Ami finds the figurine and takes it home with her.

Back home, Ami takes Ana's shirt to mend it. While sewing by the candlelight, Ami accidentally pricks her finger. The blood falls onto Pulgasari, bringing him to life in miniature form. Pulgasari eats some of Ami's sewing needles. While everyone sleeps, Pulgasari eats the lock off the door. Ami and Ana awaken to find Pulgasari in the forge, eating iron and growing bigger. Will Pulgasari be the savior that Takse asked for?

<i>30 Rock</i> fans, among others, are aware of Kim Jong Il's love of cinema and willingness to kidnap people on behalf of it. In this case, Kim kidnapped South Korean director Shin Sang-ok and his wife Choi Eun-hee. Still, Shin Sang-ok says he loved working on this movie and considers it his best. Some interpret Pulgasari's rise as a folk hero only to become its own oppressive force as a comment on Kim Il Sung's revolution and subsequent dictatorship. Shin claims any political messages are unintentional.

The vibes in this movie are immaculate, even though it is quite cheap. The sets and costumes aren't much, but they get the job done. The acting is wooden, fit for the exposition-heavy script.  The score gets a little wacky and playful in places, giving the movie a kids' movie feel, despite all the blood and torture. The horse skinning scenes (yes, "scenes" plural) were very intense!

Still, I like the movie! It moves and is entertaining. I love the design for Pulgasari. He feels like a more traditional rendering of what Godzilla might have looked like. I also love that he starts figurine-sized and grows bigger when he eats metal. Oh, and when he gets hot and glows red looks sick as hell. Watching him march alongside the rebels is so satisfying! The historical aspects of the movie give off Kurosawa vibes, but, like, obviously not at the same level.
