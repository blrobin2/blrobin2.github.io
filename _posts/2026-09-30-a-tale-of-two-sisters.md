In a psychiatric facility, a doctor interviews Su-mi to determine if she remembers what happened that day. Cut to a car pulling up to a dark, stately home. Moo-hyeon, Su-mi's father, opens the car door. Out pour Su-mi and her sister, Su-yeon. The two girls go to the pier to dip their feet in the water before heading inside.

Once inside, Eun-joo, the girls' stepmom, emerges from the darkness, chastising them for not coming straight in. Both girls are on edge around Eun-joo, especially Su-yeon. At dinner, Moo-hyeon is checked out while everyone eats in silence. Moo-hyeon sets some medicine down in front of Eun-joo, telling her to clean up tomorrow. Su-mi storms off, and Eun-joo tells Su-yeon to do what she always does and follow her sister.

That night, the father has trouble sleeping next to Eun-joo and goes downstairs to fall asleep in front of the television. While lying in bed, Su-yeon hears noises and suddenly her door opens. She hides underneath the covers. Someone pulls the covers off. When Su-yeon looks around, she sees no one. She runs to Su-mi's room, where Su-mi comforts Su-yeon and lets her sleep in her bed. Su-mi also notices scratches on Su-yeon's arms and wrist, and suspects Eun-joo.

<blockquote><i>-That woman is strange, and so is this house.</i></blockquote>

The film follows the family as we learn the secrets that they are doing their best to bury and forget.

This film is one I checked out closer to when it came out and felt totally lost, to the point where I consider this time my first "real" watch. I remember literally one shot and nothing else, so it was nice to see this with fresh eyes.

The film explores the complexities of abuse in a family setting, using Gothic horror and East Asian ghost tropes to convey the unspoken but common knowledge and the ways that people cope (or fail to). The sense of dread hits the audience the moment we enter the house, and it never lets up.

While many horror movies from the time used unreliable and partial perspectives to weave information slowly throughout the story, this film uses them to obscure and contort it, creating something that feels coherent on the surface but starts to unravel as we spend more time with it.

<blockquote><i>I'll always be with you.</i></blockquote>

The film uses striking reds throughout, from the kitchen tiles to brief moments of blood. What I believe it symbolizes may spoil some of the movie's twists and turns, so I'll leave it ambiguous for now.

Overall, I found this one quite compelling and loved how much it had me questioning my theories throughout. I'm curious to revisit it with the ending in mind to see how the experience plays out.
