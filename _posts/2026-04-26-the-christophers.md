Steven Soderbergh continues refusing to learn anything about color grading. I chose a RECOLORED banner so that you could actually see all of Michaela Coel's face. During the movie, the color was too muted and the lighting too bland to ever see her face in full. Not a problem for the white actors.

Once I allowed myself not to feel the heat from that complaint, I was able to enjoy the story. It's very <i>Finding Forrester</i> (what do you expect from the writer of <i>Now You See Me</i>, <i>Men in Black</i>, and the 90s <i>Super Mario Bros</i> movie?), but Michaela Coel and Ian McKellen's performances elevated the material to a satisfying place. It seems James Corden was directed to play close to self, and it's one of his better performances, as I hated his guts.

One of the smarter choices in the movie was also a tad disappointing — we never see any of the art pieces very closely or for very long (with one notable exception at the end). It's good, because we know that the actors aren't making this work, but when so much of the movie is about the final product, I kind of wanted to see what that actually was. If I'm seeing this in a theater, imagine how much more difficult it will be when it drops on streaming in a couple of weeks.

Overall, I'm glad I saw it and enjoyed it. I think seeing anything with Owen adds some joy to the experience, though
