William rides in the back of Solo's cab in Winston-Salem, North Carolina. Solo laughs, surprising William. Solo says it would cost $200 for him to drive William to Blowing Rock. William offers $1000 for Solo to take him on October 20th and leave him there. Solo realizes that William is serious. William gives Solo a $100 down payment and will pay the rest that day. Solo gives William his card so he can call Solo directly. Tonight, Solo drops him off at the movies.

Solo finishes his shift and returns to dispatch early in the morning. Solo asks dispatch that, if William calls, they give the ride to Solo. He gets a ride home, where he greets his wife and stepdaughter, Alex. That night, Solo picks up William again to take him to the movies. This time, William has his bags — he sold his apartment and is moving into a motel. Solo tries to give William the money back, but William refuses.

They make it to the movie late, so Solo takes William to the hotel, making some stops along the way. At a convenience store, Solo picks up Roc, one of his "preferred clients," and makes some stops for him. They also stop at a bar, where Solo picks up William's beers while he and Roc play pool and make plans for a bigger client. Instead of dropping William off at the hotel, Solo brings him home to meet his family.

The film follows Solo and William's relationship as Solo tries to remind William about what makes life worth living.

Solo is from Senegal. He discusses his cultural tradition of taking care of your family, no matter what. The only reason he's in the US is to make money to send back home, and he plans to return to Senegal when he's old so his family can care for him. He doesn't understand that in the US, family isn't like that. We are raised on the misguided notion of individual freedom, which seems to mean freedom from the burden of responsibility for how our actions affect others.

William doesn't want to see his family. He is a closed book who doesn't give a fuck about anyone. Solo doesn't relent, and William is too depressed to fight. Solo works his way into William's life, partly because of his own troubles. He stays in William's hotel from time to time to study for an interview to become a flight attendant. Slowly, Solo pulls bits and pieces of William's life, and it becomes clear just how much Solo needs William.

The film is essentially <a href="https://boxd.it/5m4tXx"><i>Taste of Cherry</i></a>, transplanted to North Carolina. It doesn't have the same poetry as Karostami's brilliant meditation on suicide, but its tight focus on character gives the film a distinct flavor. Souléymane Sy Savané and Red West are the only two people in the movie who have any acting ability. The momentum really rests on Savané's charisma — it's a shame he hasn't had bigger opportunities, as his best-known roles are as "African Cab Driver" and "West African Man."

I grew up in Boone, the closest town to Blowing Rock. It seems the only reason the filmmakers chose this location was the legend around Blowing Rock of a Cherokee man jumping off and a gust of wind returning him. This film is too grounded to explore that with any curiosity, which makes me question why they chose the location.
