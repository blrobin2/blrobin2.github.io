Kyle Reese never knew Earth before Judgment Day. As a child, he was rescued by a man named John Connor and brought into the resistance against the machines. John leads an infiltration mission into what appears to be a standard machine encampment, but John assures Kyle it contains a powerful weapon. Meanwhile, the Colorado unit engages Skynet's core and destroys it once and for all. But they were too late.

The machines, knowing they were losing, had used the tactical time weapon to send a T-800 back to Los Angeles in 1984 to kill John Connor's mother, Sarah, thereby preventing John from being born and undoing every victory the humans have achieved. Kyle volunteers to go, having carried a picture of Sarah and knowing her through John's stories. Kyle knows she won't be the warrior John told him about yet. But what the humans did not anticipate was that the machines had an inside man among them.

The T-800 arrives in 1984 and tries to steal some clothes off of some punks, but before he can, another T-800 arrives to stop him. When Kyle arrives, he steals an unhoused man's pants, but while running from the cops, he is stopped by a T-1000 dressed as a cop. When it looks like all hope is lost, Sarah Connor arrives in an armored truck, telling Kyle to come with her if he wants to live.

This ain't your daddy's Terminator!

This film takes the curious yet predictable approach of soft-rebooting the whole series. Like practically all reboots from this era, the filmmakers fill the movie with references to and shot-for-shot recreations of moments from the previous films while creating an alternate timeline.

What I find most fascinating is that this film explores with Pops what Star Trek does with Data. Pops is the only Terminator we've seen age significantly. In the process, he has learned to make jokes and connect with meaningful emotional states. In other words, the film looks at things from the machine's POV for the first time in the series. We understand that beneath that death-driven programming lies the capacity to experience the world.

The problem is that the action, while solid in places, becomes the same endless, CGI-laden chase fest that made <i><a href="https://boxd.it/2aES">T3</a></i> so tedious. And while it sometimes makes amusing jokes, it also does dumb shit like needle-drop the Cops theme song when they get arrested.

Actually, I think nostalgia is genuinely tinging my perspective on this movie. Seeing Arnold in anything feels kind of nice, even when it kind of sucks. I'm charmed, and will definitely overrate this.
