<b><a href="https://boxd.it/q5w9E/detail" rel="nofollow">Criterion Challenge 2021 | 39/52 | Directed by John Cassavetes</a></b>

Richard Forst arrives at a financing meeting, irritable as usual. His secretary and employees cater to his every need. He meets with Nita Whiteford and her associates regarding a new film — the "Dolce Vita of the commercial space."

After the meeting, Richard gathers at The Losers Club with his friend Freddie, where they meet a sex worker named Jeannie. Drunk and noisy, they bring her to the car and drive back to Jeannie's place. Jeannie tolerates their drunken dancing. Drunken tears. Lamenting the days when they didn't have to worry about their wives and kids.

Freddie whines when Richard gets more of Jeannie's attention, asking her how much she charges, souring everyone's mood. He apologizes and leaves. Richard, his illusions dispelled, also leaves. He returns home to his wife, Maria, and has dinner. Maria doesn't like Fred anymore—Fred's wife says he talks in his sleep and reveals that he cheats on his wife.

The next morning, Richard asks Maria for a divorce. The film follows the fallout.

<blockquote><i>Yeah, I am silly little puddin'.</i></blockquote>

The film explores the routines we enact to evade intimacy — song and dance, jokes, liquor, dancing—the faces we wear: laughter in public, tears when alone. But if we stopped and admitted how unhappy we are with each other, maybe we could help each other out. Or maybe our lives might change.

All the men are rich, shitty, and miserable — cheating men tend to be that way. And like all shitty men, they put their problems on the women around them. They demand levity, then run back to routine when the levity is too much.

<blockquote><i>Definition of "serious": blah, blah, blah, blah.</i></blockquote>Gena Rowlands gives the standout performance, as per usual. Lynn Carlin also does a tremendous job. I despise John Marley's character, so he's doing something right. This might be my favorite Seymour Cassel performance — he's so wacky.

The verite camera style works great here. The hyper-zoom reaction cutaways do a lot of wonderful storytelling. Cinematographer/Editor Al Ruban has always smashed it out of the park.

After watching some of <a href="https://boxd.it/fe9eHH" rel="nofollow">Cassavetes</a>' <a href="https://boxd.it/3tOaXx" rel="nofollow">masterpieces</a>, these less structured experiments are a bit exhausting. It feels like every ensemble scene starts with people laughing at nonsense, then someone gets angry and spoils everything. Some more effort in the structure department would do this movie wonders.

So much singing! (derogatory)
