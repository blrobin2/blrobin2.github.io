Cold open with the guitar hitting the F9, and three boys, John, George, and Ringo, run from a manic crowd to the tune of "A Hard Day's Night." On a park bench sits Paul, wearing a fake goatee disguise so he can see his grandfather, John, onto the train. Once grandfather John boards his train, Paul removes the disguise and joins in the escape.

They find the car with Grandfather John, who's joining the trip because he's "nursing a broken heart." Their road manager, Shake, bursts in with snacks. The band manager, Norm, also joins them after a failed attempt to eat a sandwich. He appeals to the boys to be ordinary and respectable for their TV appearance. An older gentleman joins the car, closing the window and shouting at them for playing music on the radio.

Once the boys arrive in London, they must dodge the mob waiting, diving into a taxi before being torn limb from limb. The film follows the boys as they prepare for their TV performance and all the wacky hijinks in between.

<blockquote><i>— Do you often see your father?<br>
— No, we're just good friends.</i></blockquote>

The rapid editing cut to the beat of the music had not been as fully explored as in this, making this film a precursor to the music video craze. The format of four mischievous bandmates getting into hijinks inspired the creation of the Monkees' TV show — nearly every show featuring boys in a band follows the same characterization and structure. The film borrows a verité quality that was prevalent in the French New Wave, giving everything a boisterous energy and feeling of being there with the band.

Screenwriter Alun Owen knew how to write in the Scouse dialect, so his lines fit well in the boys' mouths. While they don't have archetypes per se, each boy gets a chance at being the smartest person in the room, as well as the silliest. The script is full of cheeky one-liners and goofy little gags, but importantly, nothing too broad or arch. This film is the first of its kind to present a pop group as fictionalized versions of themselves.

The album that serves as the soundtrack for the movie is also the first album of theirs to contain all original songs. And they're all pretty perfect pop tunes. Hearing "A Hard Day's Night" in the opening gave me such immense chills. The closest experience I've had to that is hearing "Feel It All Around" in the opening credits to Portlandia. The frenetic cutaway for "Can't Buy Me Love" is iconic. And the instrumental orchestral arrangements from George Martin are delightful.

No one says "The Beatles" once in this movie. I mean, it's on the movie poster and Ringo's drum set. But if there's anything you can't do in movies anymore, it's assume you're big enough to never say your band's name.
