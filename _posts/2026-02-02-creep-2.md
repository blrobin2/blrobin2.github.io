Josef, now going by Aaron, is going through a midlife crisis — he's grown out his hair and beard, he's drinking green smoothies, and murdering people just doesn't have the same panache it used to. So, he calls upon Sara, who focuses on making work about lonely men, to document his story. In the process, he finds Sara not to be as easy to manipulate as the people he has killed in the past.

When making a sequel to a somewhat meta found footage movie, what else can you do but go even more meta? And what better way to do that than through Sara? She is satisfying in her tough-as-nails approach, but the movie never shies away from her vulnerability. She has learned many strategies for dealing with this type of man. But in the process, she's decided she has to do it alone.

In the first <i><a href="https://boxd.it/cWTQhB">Creep</a></i>, we saw a man who thought his gender was enough to keep him safe. In this movie, we see a woman who thought that she had learned everything she needed to survive. In both cases, they are fundamentally lonely people without a community. If they had people in their lives, then it is likely they never would've ended up in Josef/Aaron's path.

While I enjoyed aspects of <i>Creep</i>, I feel like this movie improves in many ways: its sense of humor, its pacing, and the subtle performances from Mark Duplass and Desiree Akhavan. Akhavan, in particular, really ramps up the experience by giving us someone we really want to root for. I had a blast!
