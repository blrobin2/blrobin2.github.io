<blockquote><i>A long time ago, in a high school not so far away…</i></blockquote>

Woody's alarm goes off. So do Spider's and Dave's, waking up both Dave and his brother, Wendell. They each pull cash from their respective hiding places — Playboy, a copy of Lolita, or their parents' wallet. Woody, Spider, and Dave are planning on taking a trip to Tijuana after class. Wendell begs to come along so he can buy some fireworks. Perlman was going, but his parents grounded him, so Dave reluctantly lets Wendell come along, so long as he chips in for the trip.

And why are these boys going to Tijuana? To lose their virginity, of course, much to Woody's girlfriend, Carol's, dismay. They stop at the gas station and encounter gas station clerk Kathy fighting with her husband, Larry, who is clearly sleeping with someone else. The boys shoplift a bunch of food while the couple fights. Kathy tries to drive off in the company truck, but accidentally crashes into the storefront. Horrified, she asks the boys to let her ride with them.

When Kathy finds out where the boys are going, she decides she is going to divorce Larry in Tijuana. Hijinks ensue!

I have no idea why we're supposed to root for these horrible boys. Dave is the grossest by a mile — besides getting laid, his big project is buying Spanish Fly so he can SA some women. Spider is the most needlessly cruel, causing almost all of the trouble just by fighting and shit-talking. Regardless, they are objectively the villains of this story, no matter whose perspective you take.

The only reason this movie works at all is that Shelley Long has comedic chops and her character has something resembling emotional depth. But she later has a romantic entanglement with Tom Cruise, and it's… awkward.

I feel like any boys who drove down to Tijuana to pay for sex were probably seen as total losers by their classmates. Like, develop an emotional connection with a girl your own age, or get really repressed about it like other kids who grew up in a fundamentalist church.

In terms of the filmmaking, it's quite well pieced together for a non-union job. Gilbert Taylor doesn't do shoddy camerawork. But, 1 hour 40 minutes for this story? Seriously?

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- There are several "raunchy" songs by Johnny Valby, performing as Johnny Hotrocks. They're all, surprise, gross.
- At least one of the locals gets to dunk on them for coming to Tijuana and ruining his home, but they don't get enough justice.
- This climactic car chase probably ate up the majority of the budget.
- The Joe Spinneli jump scare!
- Somehow, it ends in a food fight.
