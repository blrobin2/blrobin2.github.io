<b><a href="https://boxd.it/qaTwm/detail">Asian Cinema Challenge 2023 | 52/52 | Wild Card</a></b>

A truck drives through town advertising Shimiyuza Supermarket's first-anniversary sale, offering 50% off all store items. While driving, they pass by a humble grocery store run by Reiko Moritaya. Reiko, a war widow, runs the shop for her in-laws, who cannot compete with the supermarket's prices. Other small mom-and-pop shops are also facing similar concerns with lower sales.

That night at the bar, Reiko's brother-in-law, Kōji, gets into a bar fight with Shimiyuza, who brags about his 5¥ eggs and is content to waste them on things like egg-eating competitions. The police call Reiko's store and tell her to come pick up Kōji. Reiko's mother-in-law asks about  Kōji, and Reiko lies to cover for him. This isn't the first time Reiko has been in this position.

Hisako, Reiko's sister-in-law, thinks Kōji is lazy because Reiko does all the work. Hisako has spoken to Mother, insisting that they let Reiko go. Mother shows Reiko a picture of a widower, encouraging her to remarry. Reiko doesn't want to remarry and considers herself a member of the family. But when the subject of Kōji getting married in the future comes up, Reiko relents.

The film follows Reiko and Kōji through this transitional time in their life, and the unspoken feelings that finally come to the surface.

The film explores themes of sacrifice and time wasted. Kōji and Mother believe Reiko has sacrificed important time in her life in service to the family. Mother is thankful for Reiko, though she also pities her after 18 years with the family. Meanwhile, Mother and Reiko see Kōji, a college graduate at 25, content to be jobless and aimless. Still, Reiko isn't worried about Kōji.

It also explores the nature of exploitation and its effects on small communities just trying to get by. Shimiyuza could easily sell his items at a more competitive price, but instead insists on setting prices so low that it drives other shops out of business. At one point early on, one of the store owners hangs himself, unable to face the harsh conditions to come. And many of these folks went through the harsh post-war conditions, so it's not as though they aren't resilient. They are just running out of options.

As the film progresses, we see how the supermarket's exploitation bleeds into the Moritayas' concerns. The sisters, Hisako and Takako, want to push Reiko out of the family. Kōji looks into the store becoming a supermarket, but only on the condition that Reiko holds an executive position. Hisako is dead-set that, if they become a supermarket, Reiko cannot be involved, her and her husband thinking through the percentage of profit they want to make.

The film takes a leisurely pace but never feels slow. A calmness colors most scenes — the emotional undercurrents are powerful yet unknowable. It is a masterclass in how people speak based on what they know, talking past one another as they try to uncover the truth. This becomes most apparent with Kōji and Reiko.

The title suggests some of what is going on, but it's important to know the original Japanese title, 乱れる, which means "to be confused." These are two people who thought they knew the direction their lives would take, but circumstances changed everything.

Overall, the film is a beautiful melodrama that does what Naruse does best: finding people on the periphery of life and looking at them closely for a little while. Here, we look at a widow who built a humble life out of what remained — to most, she is simply the woman who ran a local grocery. To those close to her, she is the rock that holds the family together.
