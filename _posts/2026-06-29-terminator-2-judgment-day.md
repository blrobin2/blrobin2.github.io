I have always found this movie less enjoyable than the original, if only because the original has such a specific tone and aesthetic that this movie totally throws away, much like how <i>Aliens</i> wasn't <i>Alien</i> — not an inherently bad thing, but a hurdle for me. Also, this one definitely doubles down on the "one special person" historical theory with both John Connor and Miles Dyson.

That said, whether it's nostalgia or James Cameron's direction, this one has some emotional beats that work for me. He always seemed to know that extra runtime for character is always gonna improve an action movie (when done effectively).

I love the <b>eXtreme DVD</b> version my dad gave me that sat on his shelf for years, totally unopened. I don't love the menu's use of "sensory control" to label the sound and subtitles section. It has an "Interactive Mode" where it shoots factoids at you in a Pop-Up Video-style throughout. It's also a "special edition" with additional footage, including the CPU reset surgery and character beats.

#### Stray Thoughts / Spoilers

- The concept of this movie is already so silly. They sent a Terminator back in time to kill Sarah Connor, and it failed. So, instead of sending 20 terminators to wipe her out, or killing John Connor as a baby, they wait until he's a teenager and do it again.
- The fiery playground credits rule.
- Is it really easier to steal clothes from a person than a store?
- The "Bad to the Bone" music cue is hilarious.
- The movie convinces us initially that the T-1000 is a good guy by having him kill a cop
- Always happy to see Xander Berkeley
- Thank you, Dr. Silberman, for the first movie recap
- Just think how fit Sarah Connor would've gotten if she had halfway decent psychological support and care?
- The playground explosion scared the shit out of me as a kid, especially Sarah turning into a skeleton.
- I like the loop that Cyberdyne makes robots because one of their robots goes back in time and his remnants teach them how to do it.
- Tim's a real one for lying to the cop, no questions asked. All the other kids in this movie are such narcs!
- Of course the T-1000 effects are sick
- The car stunts are so ridiculous
- What really makes this movie is Edward Furlong's Bart Simpson impersonation
- The T-1000 can't replicate complex shapes, but it can replicate humans perfectly.
- The psych ward workers are so disgusting — obviously, so we enjoy seeing them get their asses handed to them.
- I love that they use the twin trick twice in this movie — once for Sarah Connor, and another for the cop getting coffee.
- I used to think Sarah looked scary-jacked when I was a kid, but superhero movie regimens have been so absurd that it isn't as shocking. Don't get me wrong, she's hella thin and shredded.
- "He'll live" after shooting the security guard in the kneecaps.
- The T-1000 is awfully gentle to the woman security guard.
- I love when Sarah pulls the gun from the Terminator's hip.
- "Are we learning yet?"
- The smile is funny.
- Miles' characterization is probably the best of the series — earnest, to a fault, but ready to throw away his life's work to save humanity.
- Sarah realizing she will never have a normal family life as she wishes is so heartbreaking
- "NO FATE"
- The liquid nitrogen scene
- The third act really leans on T-1000 being slow and sadistic with his kill attempts.
- Giving one last thumbs up is so silly.
- The post-credits scene is crazy.
