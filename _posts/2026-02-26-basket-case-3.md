<b>**_ SPOILERS for Basket Case 1 and 2 are inevitable. If you haven't seen those movies, this is all going to sound quite unhinged. Because it is. _**</b>

We open with a recap of the final moments of Basket Case 2. Belial and Eve are having sex in the attic. Susan reveals Bernard, the monstrous baby that has been in her womb for the past six years. Duane accidentally pushes Susan out the window, killing her and Bernard. Granny Ruth sics the freaks onto Duane. Duane decides that, to make things right, he must reattach Belial. After forcibly sewing Belial back to his side, Duane believes everything will be okay again.

Months later, we find Duane reseparated from Belial, straightjacketed in a Granny Ruth's padded room. Granny Ruth releases Duane, who seems to have forgotten what has happened to him. Belial has gone silent on Duane. They prepare for the birth of Belial and Eve's progeny. Because of the unprecedented nature of Eve's pregnancy, Ruth and the gang will travel to Georgia to see Uncle Hal, her ex-husband. The group boards the school bus labeled "No One You Know" and hits the road.

Sheriff Andrew Griffin stops by Uncle Hal's estate in Georgia, dropping off some old science equipment for Little Hal. Once inside, a Rube Goldberg of a cart rolls up and offers the sheriff coffee — you know, 90s movies loved their complicated inventions for simple tasks. Over the mantle is a painting of a baby with six arms. Uncle Hal and the Sheriff catch up on pleasantries. As the Sheriff leaves, he sees Little Hal standing on the upstairs balcony, waving him off.

What's going on with Belial? Will he and Duane make up? What will happen with Eve's pregnancy?

<i><a href="https://boxd.it/6J4CFx">Basket Case 2</a></i> and this movie were made within a year of one another, making the aesthetics more cohesive than in the gap between 1982's <i>Basket Case</i> and the sequel. With each sequel, the movies have grown progressively more campy. Henenlotter's 80s output was much more transgressive and a harder sell than these movies' silliness. That's mostly due to the deal that he made with the financiers to remove the gorier, unrated stuff he had planned for this one.

The monster designs are so wacky. I love them so much! Pretty much all the monster designs are the same ones from <i>Basket Case 2</i>, but they get more screen time and become more central to the story. It's hard for me to hate a movie with practical effect monsters — they're like the cantina scene in <i>Star Wars</i>, but with a more <i>Beetlejuice</i>/<i>Hellraiser</i> bent.

By this point, you're either along for the ride or you abandoned ship sometime during the last movie. And I wouldn't blame you, to be honest. I can't say I'm wild about this one, but it goes down easy enough. The film picks up about halfway through, when the police start snooping around — chaos and violence ensue. But it's all a little too half-baked for me.
