<blockquote><i>In Yugoslavia, not too long ago, it was believed that families without male heirs were cursed. In order to break the curse, a family would pick one of the girls to be a "Virgina." This story is about one of those girls, born at the end of the 19th century, not far from the Adriatic Sea.</i></blockquote>

Timotije tills the rocky soil, sweating and exhausted. His pregnant wife, Dostana, prays for a good crop and for a son as she too works outside. In the shade behind them, their three daughters watch. A group of boys crests the hillside, singing blessings for good crops. That night, in the barn, Dostana lays out a cloth while the daughters prep the hot water and scissors.

Dostana squats to give birth while Timotije pours one out for St. George, praying that his son has a better destiny. Dostana gives birth, then breaks out in tears as she realizes that the baby is another girl. Timotije loads a gun and brings the girl into the field. Holding the gun to the baby's head, a strong wind slaps against him. And so, he raises his gun to the heavens and shoots, sparing the baby's life.

And so, Timotije declares the baby a Virgina, and she will be raised as a boy named Stevan until Dostana "gets it right" and has a boy. The town criers announce that Timotije has finally had a boy. People believe God has abandoned this family. Neighbors come to bless Timotije and the son. Outside, immigrants leave for America as the rain pours for their crops. The family sleeps uneasily.

As Steven grows up, we watch the family cut her hair, bind her chest, and check her muscles as she works the field to ensure she passes as a man to those outside the family.

<blockquote><i>You're better off as a man. Just look at me. Better to be a rooster for a day than a hen for a lifetime.</i></blockquote>

The film explores patriarchal societal roles through Stevan, who moves through society with the same privileges as men, including being head of household, moving freely in social situations, and doing "men's" work. Early on, we see Stevan experience bitterness from her sisters, but she can freely boss them around without reproach.

Still, Timotije forces Stevan to remain constrained within the walls of their farm. If Stevan disobeys, Timotije whips Stevan and demands that she shed no tears, because men do not cry. Stevan is given a toy gun to play with, but quietly takes her sister's dolls to play with when her father isn't looking. And most difficult, Stevan falls for a male friend.

<blockquote><i>Stevan, if you only knew what life awaits you.</i></blockquote>

The term Virgina literally means "virgin," but refers here to a third gender role within certain Balkan cultures, where AFAB people take a vow of chastity and live out their lives recognized as men. Though the practice died out in most Baltic countries, and may have ceased during communism, the collapse of a sustainable government likely encouraged a resurgence. No exact number exists, but experts estimate around twelve were still alive as of 2022.

The landscapes are absolutely beautiful, capturing the Dalmatian Hinterland and the rugged Dinaric Alps. Formally, this film does very little that we haven't seen before. Still, I appreciate this film's exploration of gender, even if it does so with a heavy hand.
