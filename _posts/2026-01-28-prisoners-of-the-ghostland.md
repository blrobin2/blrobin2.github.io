Nic Cage (I guess his name in this movie is Hero? I might just call him Nic Cage instead) robs the Bleufeur National Bank with his heist partner, Psycho. Bernice and two geishas, Stella and Nanci, escape from an okiya in Samurai Town, out into Ghostland.

Cut to Samurai Town, a weird blend of Wild West and Japanese culture. For example, they have an Old West sheriff station with samurai swords crossed in front of the sign and neon lights everywhere. People in the streets wear either Japanese attire (traditional or contemporary) or cowboy stuff. The station has Nic Cage imprisoned in only a mawashi. The Governor, who runs Samurai Town, arrives and declares that Nic Cage not only robbed the bank but also killed a bunch of innocent civilians.

The Governor's right hand, Yasujiro, fetches a leather suit lined with explosives at the neck and testicles for Nic Cage. The Governor's granddaughter, Bernice, is missing. So, he hires Nic Cage to go into the Ghostland to find her. If he strays, messes with Bernice, or doesn't rescue her in time, the suit will explode. But if he succeeds, he walks away a free man. The Governor carries the keys to the suit around his neck.

So, Nic Cage jumps in the provided Toyota Celica, does a donut, exits the car, and jumps on a bicycle instead. Will Nic Cage succeed? Did he really kill all those people?

You may also be asking, "What's the Ghostland?" The movie doesn't tell us straight away, but it seems to be a Mad Max-esque wasteland with all sorts of irradiated what-have-yous and what-nots. There seem to be several different tribes/clans/cults, but I don't know what their deals are beyond elaborate costumes. About halfway through, one of these groups puts on a show that tells the story, so I won't spoil it.

The film seems like just a mashup of things the filmmakers thought would be fun to throw in. Every moment seems written around the thought, "Wouldn't that be kookie?" It's a little amusing at first, but I was waiting for it to all gel at some point. The movie regularly stops for dream sequences or surreal imagery.

There's a theme about reclaiming one's voice as one's own. The Ghostland seems to be a metaphor for depression — those who get lost in it lose something, such as their will to live. A demon murders anyone who tries to escape.

There's also this thing with time — The Governor is obsessed with time, saying it waits for no one. The first Ghostland settlement Nic Cage visits has people pulling the minute hand down on a large clock tower with a rope to prevent time from advancing and, by extension, another disaster from occurring. Disaster means another nuclear bomb.

I guess that, because of Sion Sono's heart attack during pre-production, they sort of rushed this thing. It doesn't really come together despite its best effort to gel. Unfortunately, this movie is the first of his that I've watched, but don't worry, I'll be checking out his other stuff in due time.

Also, I don't know why this gets categorized as horror. Nothing about this is horror movie adjacent.

#### Stray Thoughts/Spoilers

- Nic Cage seems drunk the whole movie. He talks with a slur and stumbles around a whole bunch.
- The bank has people dressed in gumball colors to complement the boy standing in front of the large gumball machine.
- It feels weird to have Nic Cage calling all these Japanese people "vermin."
- "No! I will karate chop you!"
- "Hi-fucking-ya!"
- When Nic Cage loses a testicle, he runs around hooting and hollering, then holds it up before passing out.
- How many times are we going to watch this gumball machine explode?
- The cult leader, Enoch, seems to be reading from Wuthering Heights.
- I guess the machine gun climax is a nod to <a href="https://boxd.it/b1BwPV"><i>Duck, You Sucker!</i></a>
