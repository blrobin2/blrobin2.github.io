Black skyscrapers of steel and concrete tear through the clouds. Computers and the desire for order in the chaos keep the people blind and docile, unable to see the unexplained phenomena all around them. One such phenomenon is the Black World, where evil and demons run wild. In Tokyo, at a night coffee bar, Taki receives money from the bartender — a lost bet regarding a woman named Kanako, with whom Taki has been trying to hook up for three months. Tonight, he's going home with her.

In the bathroom, Kanako touches up her lipstick. But in the background, we see another hand with the same long red nails as Kanako, passed out on the floor. Taki and Kanako take a taxi back to her high-rise apartment. Before Taki knows it, Kanako is taking off her black lingerie. They have long, passionate sex. When Taki passes out on Kanako, her limbs elongate, turning into spider tarsi. This spider woman has what she came for: Taki's semen.

The next day, Taki is sore at his job at an electronics company in Shibuya. He meets with his boss, and the two discuss the Black World treaty, which will be renewed soon for another 500 years. In secret, Taki is a Black Guard, and his boss assigns him the duty of ensuring the treaty is ratified by protecting a signatory until the ceremony tomorrow. He will also work with a Black Guard from the other side — a woman named Makie.

With illegal agents from the Black World trying to stop the treaty signing at any cost, will Taki be able to complete his assignment?

I chose this movie because it kept coming up for anime and J-horror, and so I was curious to see how that played out. The movie is full of unusual demons and monsters, but the worldbuilding is so clunky and uninteresting.

The film has that unchecked misogyny of movies like <i>Blade Runner</i> escalated to Japanese pervert levels. Every woman, even (especially) Makie, is objectified. The signatory, Giuseppe, is the worst example of this, feeling up and ogling Makie, propositioning her for sex, all against her consent. The baddies fight Taki and rape Makie, and in the latter's case, it's always depicted as though it is inexplicably pleasurable to her.

What a frustrating experience! You could take many of these elements and present them differently to make something quite compelling. The climax throws a twist that attempts to justify what we see on screen, but it just doesn't work.
