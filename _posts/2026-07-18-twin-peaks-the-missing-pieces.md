Agents Desmond and Stanley hit up Hap's for a bite to eat. Hap is dead, but they ask Jack about Teresa Banks, who used to work there. Never saw her with friends, but they should ask Irene. The lamp flickers and shoots sparks. The agents and Irene leave at daybreak.

Sheriff Cable demonstrates his ability to bend iron. He takes off his badge, and Desmond does the same. The two fight, the other police and staff watching and laughing. That is, until Desmond gets the upper hand.

At the FBI, Agent Cooper talks to Diane, offscreen, about something she's changed in the office. He meets up with Agent Stanley. Stanley doesn't know what the blue rose means. He shows Cooper the letter found under Teresa's fingernail.

Mr. Jeffries checks in at the Palm Deluxe. The power lines screech. The Man from Another Place talks to Bob and the other ephemera of darkness — the creamed corn. The ring is in the black lodge. Laura awaits.

And so the film goes, taking us to the periphery and recesses of scenes and moments from <i>Fire Walk with Me</i>.

<blockquote><i>I have the fury of my own momentum.</i></blockquote>

I'm glad I remember as much of <i>Fire Walk with Me</i> as I do, because this movie makes no effort to ground the audience or remind them.

I can see why these scenes were cut from the movie. Not that any of it is bad — it's all quite fascinating, actually. I would watch a version of the movie with these scenes reintegrated, just because I love being in this world.

But besides the black lodge stuff and the extended Bowie scenes, a lot of this feels like the TV show. Part of <i>Fire Walk with Me</i>'s power lies in the absence of the sillier/lighter fare from the TV show. Also, if you're anyone other than a diehard fan who can't get enough Lynch/Twin Peaks, you won't get much from this.

The one scene I think should have been in the movie is the Doc Hayward scene — it’s so beautiful and adds resonance to later scenes.

I don't think I will score this, just because it isn't a coherent movie on its own.
