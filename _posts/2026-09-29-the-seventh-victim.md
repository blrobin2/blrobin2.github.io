<b><a href="https://boxd.it/q5w9E/detail">Criterion Challenge 2021 | 43/52 | Queersighted: Queer Fear</a></b>

<blockquote><i>I runne to death, and death meets me as fast, and all my pleasures are like yesterday.</i> — Jonne Donne, Holy Sonnett VII</blockquote>

Mary Gibson, a student at Highcliffe Academy, knocks on the principal's door. The principal informs Mary that Jacqueline, Mary's sister and only known relative, has gone missing. In her absence, Mary's tuition is delinquent. The principal offers Mary money to travel to New York City to find her. Should she fail, she can always come back to Highcliffe.

<blockquote><i>One must have courage to live in the world.</i></blockquote>

In New York, Mary meets Esther Redi at La Sagesse, a cosmetics company Jacqueline once owned but has since sold to Esther. At La Sagesse, Mary runs into Frances, who saw Jacqueline about a week ago at an Italian restaurant in the Village called Dante. The restaurant owners rented a room to Jacqueline. They open the room to find it empty, save for a chair and a noose hanging from the ceiling.

Mary files a missing person's report at the police department. There, PI Irving August offers to help Mary and tells her to go to the morgue. After Mary leaves, a Mr. Radeaux tells August to back off. Instead, August gets the file from the police. The morgue sends Mary to a man named Gregory Ward, who loves Jacqueline and seems to know more than he lets on.

<blockquote><i>Because I loved Jacqueline, I thought I knew her.</i></blockquote>

The film follows Mary as she tracks down her sister. Wherever Jacqueline is, it affects many people, and they are willing to kill to protect that secret.

What happens when we give up hope? What happens when life betrays us, and our dreams die? Those who still live must discover a new life, one that comes only from letting the old one go. But historically, some have not been given that chance, chased into darkness and kept there in fear.

Tom Conway appears as Dr. Louis Judd, the psychiatrist from <i><a href="https://boxd.it/3VHTDv">Cat People</a></i>, suggesting that this film is a prequel. Like <i>Cat People</i>, this film touches on queerness, placing it among the darkness. Here, the film suggests that queerness, when forced to hide, must sublimate. We meet a cult that believes life has betrayed them, and that all that they have is evil. But that isn't enough for everyone.

<blockquote><i>I was terrified. The darkness in the corner of the room. All the little noises.</i></blockquote>

Mary meets an "intimate" friend of Jacqueline's, a woman named Frances who laments Jacqueline "going out of circulation" once she met Dr. Judd. At one point, Frances admits that she was only ever happy when she was working with Jacqueline. Throughout the film, we see heterosexual couples in the background, happy and free, out in the open. They always stand in contrast to our miserable characters.

A minor character is the poet, Jason Hoag, who spends his evenings at Dante, drinking wine with the owners but never writing. He promises to help Mary find Jacqueline. Mary's presence in his life encourages him to write again. He sees himself as a Cyrano — unable to have the one he loves, but wooing her for his friend. How often have queer people served that role for their straight friends?

The film is frustrating because director Mark Robson and editor John Lockert cut four major scenes, including the full conclusion. So, as a piece of narrative cinema, this film cannot fully satisfy. But as atmospheric horror, this film joins another of Val Lewton's RKO productions, <i><a href="https://boxd.it/58bugn">I Walked with a Zombie</a></i>, in creating a tremendous mood that exists somewhere between film noir and Gothic horror.

<blockquote><i>I am alive, yet every hope I had is dead. Death can be good. Death can be happy.</i></blockquote>
